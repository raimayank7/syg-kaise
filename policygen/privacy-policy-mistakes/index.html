Privacy policy mistakes to avoid

Most small-site policies fail in the same ways. Check yours against this list; each mistake is easy to fix.

The list

  • Copied from another site: it names the wrong company, services or country.
  • Says "we never share data" while analytics or ads run.
  • Does not mention the ad network and its cookies.
  • No contact email, or an email that is never read.
  • No date, so nobody knows whether it is current.
  • Promises deletion on request but the law makes you keep invoice records.
  • Hidden in the footer in tiny grey text that no one can find.
  • Not updated after a new plugin, form or widget was added.
  • Claims compliance with a law you have not read ("fully GDPR compliant").
  • Written in legal language no visitor can follow.

How to fix them

Generate a fresh policy from what your site truly does, put the date on it, and check the list above one by one. Then set a reminder to review it every six months and whenever you add a service.

A quick test

Ask a friend who does not know your site to read the policy and answer three questions: what do you collect, who else gets it, how do I ask you to delete it? If they can answer from the page, the policy works.

A short review routine

Every six months, open your policy and your site side by side. List every outside service the site loads, check each against the policy, and update the date. If you cannot say why you collect an item of data, stop collecting it. Keep a copy of each version with its date so you can show what you said and when.

Common questions

Can a bad policy cause trouble?

A policy that is false can be treated as a misleading statement. An honest one that is missing a clause is easier to fix.
