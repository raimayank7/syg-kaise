Privacy policy for a restaurant website

Table bookings, order forms, a loyalty list and an embedded map or reels all collect information. This page starts the generator with those ticked.

A generated policy is a starting point, not legal advice. It is a general draft written from your answers. Read it, check it against what your site really does, and ask a lawyer if your case is not simple.

What do you collect or use?
Outside services you use

What restaurants collect

Names and phone numbers for bookings and delivery, addresses for orders, email for offers, and sometimes allergy or dietary notes. A dietary note can be health information, so say it is used only for your order and deleted afterwards.

Third parties to name

  • Online ordering or booking widgets.
  • Map embeds and review widgets.
  • Instagram or Facebook feeds.
  • Delivery partners that receive the customer's address.

Marketing messages

If you send offers by WhatsApp or SMS, say you only do so with permission and how to stop. Collect that permission with a tick box that is not ticked by default, and keep a note of when and how it was given.

Before you publish a restaurant policy

Check your booking widget and delivery plug-ins: they usually come with their own cookies and their own policy, and you should link to those. If you collect a birthday or anniversary for offers, tell customers why and let them skip it.

Print a short version on the menu or at the counter if you ask for phone numbers there; the web policy is where the full text lives.

Common questions

Do delivery apps change my policy?

If customers order through Swiggy, Zomato or a similar app, that app's policy covers its own data. Your policy covers what you collect yourself, for example on your own site or at the counter.

How long do I keep customer phone numbers?

Only as long as needed for the order or the permission given. Pick a period in the form and stick to it.
