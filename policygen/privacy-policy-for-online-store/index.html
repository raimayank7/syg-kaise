Privacy policy for an online store

A shop collects the most personal information of any website: names, addresses, phone numbers and payment details. This page starts the generator with orders, accounts and a payment provider ticked.

A generated policy is a starting point, not legal advice. It is a general draft written from your answers. Read it, check it against what your site really does, and ask a lawyer if your case is not simple.

Outside services you use

What to say about payments

Say plainly that card and UPI details are handled by the payment provider, not stored on your server. This is true for Razorpay, Stripe, PayPal and most gateways, and it is the first thing a worried shopper looks for. Name the provider in the policy.

Delivery and records

Orders need an address and phone number, which you share with the courier. Tell customers that. Tax and accounting laws make you keep invoice records for years, so a promise to "delete everything on request" is not true; the policy should say that records the law requires are kept.

Pair it with the other pages

  • Refund and return policy: use our refund generator.
  • Terms of use with the paid-services clause ticked.
  • A visible contact page with your business name and address.
  • A cookie policy, if you run ads or analytics.

Before you go live with a shop policy

Place the policy link in the checkout footer and next to the "place order" button, so a buyer can read it before paying. Mention the courier company and the payment gateway by name, and keep the policy consistent with your return and shipping pages.

If you remember customers' addresses for next time, say so, and let them delete saved addresses from their account page.

Common questions

Do I have to show my GST number?

GST-registered sellers must show their GSTIN on invoices; many also show it in the footer. This is a tax rule, separate from the privacy policy.

What if I use cash on delivery only?

You still collect names, addresses and phone numbers, so you still need the policy.
