Privacy policy generator

A privacy policy tells visitors what personal information your site collects, why, who else sees it and how they can ask you to delete it. Tick what applies to your site and the text below rewrites itself.

A generated policy is a starting point, not legal advice. It is a general draft written from your answers. Read it, check it against what your site really does, and ask a lawyer if your case is not simple.

What this generator does

It builds a plain-language privacy policy from clauses we wrote ourselves. Each tick adds or removes the matching paragraph: contact forms, newsletters, accounts, orders, comments, analytics, advertising and the outside services you use. The region you pick adds the rights people have there: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 for India, the GDPR and UK GDPR for Europe, and state privacy laws such as California's for the United States.

Nothing you type is sent to us. The text is made inside your browser, and only your name, email and site name are remembered on your own device so you do not have to type them again for the next policy.

How to use it

  • Fill in your site name, your name or business name and a contact email. The email must be one you really read; people will write to it.
  • Pick the region where most of your visitors live. If you are unsure, choose "All three": the policy then covers each.
  • Tick only what is true today. A policy that promises "we collect nothing" while Google Analytics runs in the background is worse than no policy.
  • Add any other service you use, such as a chat widget, in the "other services" box.
  • Copy the text, or copy it as HTML and paste it into a page called Privacy Policy. Put a link to that page in your site footer.

What to check before you publish

Read the finished text once, slowly. Yellow blanks are things the form could not know. Then compare it with what your site really does: open your pages with the browser's developer tools or a cookie checker and see which outside services load.

Update the policy whenever you add a service. The date at the top changes by itself when you copy a fresh version.

Common questions

Is a generated privacy policy legally enough?

It is a sound starting point for a small site, written in plain words. It is not legal advice and it does not know your business. If you handle sensitive data, children's data or large volumes of personal data, have a lawyer read it.

Do I need a privacy policy if I collect nothing?

Almost every site collects something, even if only server logs and cookies from an embedded video. A short honest policy is cheap, and advertising and payment services expect one.

Where do I put the policy?

On its own page, linked from the footer of every page, and from any form or sign-up box that collects information.
