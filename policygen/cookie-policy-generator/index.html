Which cookies do you use?

Why a separate cookie page

Many sites put cookies inside the privacy policy. A separate cookie policy is easier to read and easier to link from a cookie banner. It lists the kinds of cookies you use in plain words and tells the visitor how to block or delete them. Advertising networks such as Google AdSense ask for consent for visitors in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland (see our AdSense guide), and it is good practice to tell every visitor what is set.

The generator lists only what you tick. If you use no analytics, no ads and no embeds, it says so, and that is a perfectly good cookie policy.

What counts as a cookie

Besides cookies, local storage and pixels also store or read something on a visitor's device. A site that remembers dark mode in local storage, a stats script that sets an identifier, and an embedded video that sets a tracking cookie all fall under the same idea. Strictly necessary items, such as keeping a shopping cart, usually need no consent. Statistics and advertising usually do where European rules apply.

A cookie policy is not a banner

The policy is the explanation. If your visitors are in Europe or the UK you also need a way to let them accept or refuse non-essential cookies before they load, and to change their mind later. A simple banner tool or your ad network's consent message does that. Add a link to this page in the banner.

Common questions

Do I need a cookie banner in India?

The DPDP Act, 2023 and Rules, 2025 speak of notice and consent for personal data and do not mention cookie banners as such. Google asks AdSense publishers for consent messages for visitors in the EEA, UK and Switzerland. See our DPDP and AdSense guides.

How do I find my cookies?

Open your site in Chrome, press F12, choose Application and then Cookies. Free online cookie scanners also list them. Compare the list with what you tick here.

How often should I update it?

Whenever you add or remove a service that sets cookies, and at least once a year.
