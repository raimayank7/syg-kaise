Cookie consent banners explained

Cookie banners are the pop-ups on almost every site. This guide explains what they are for, when you need one and how to avoid the patterns regulators dislike.

When you need a banner

Google requires AdSense publishers to obtain legally valid consent for cookies and similar storage and for ad personalisation from visitors in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland (see our AdSense guide, which cites the Google policy). Whether a banner is needed under the law of a particular country depends on that law, which this page does not set out; ask a lawyer for a site with many visitors from Europe. If your site sets only necessary items, such as a remembered dark-mode setting, consent is generally not what you are asked for.

What a fair banner does

  • Explains in one sentence what the cookies are for.
  • Has "Accept" and "Reject" buttons that look equally easy to press (our view of good practice, not a rule we checked).
  • Does not load the optional cookies until the visitor says yes.
  • Lets the visitor change their mind later with a link in the footer.
  • Links to the cookie policy.

Patterns to avoid

  • Pre-ticked boxes.
  • A huge Accept button and a tiny grey Reject link.
  • Treating "keep scrolling" as consent.
  • Loading the analytics script first and asking afterwards.

How to set one up

Free consent tools exist, and ad networks provide their own message. Choose one, test it by opening your site in a private window, refusing, and checking that no advertising cookies appear in the browser's Application tab. Then write the cookie policy with our generator and link it from the banner.

Common questions

Can I just hide the banner for non-EU visitors?

Some tools do, based on location. That is allowed, but the visitor's location is itself guessed, so be careful.

Do I need a banner for Google Analytics?

That depends on the law that applies to your visitors, which this page does not cover. Many sites with European visitors ask first. Ask a lawyer for a firm answer.

General starting point, not legal advice. Facts about laws were last read on 13 November 2026; check the official text for your case.
