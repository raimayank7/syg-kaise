Terms of use, privacy policy and disclaimer compared

Site builders ask for "legal pages" without saying which. These three do different jobs. Here is what each is for and which your site needs.

At a glance

  • Privacy policy: how you handle personal information. Needed by almost every site.
  • Terms of use: the rules for using the site and the limits of your responsibility. Useful for sites with accounts, comments, sales or valuable content.
  • Disclaimer: what your content is and is not, for example not professional advice. Important for advice, health, money and affiliate sites.

Which site needs which

  • Simple blog with ads: privacy policy, disclaimer, and a cookie notice.
  • Blog with comments or a forum: add terms of use.
  • Online shop: privacy, terms, refund and shipping policies.
  • Portfolio site: a short privacy policy is enough.
  • App or software: privacy, terms and, for business customers, a data-processing agreement.

Placement

Link all of them from the footer of every page, under a heading such as "Legal". Link the privacy policy and terms from any sign-up or checkout form. Keep names plain: "Privacy Policy", "Terms of Use", "Disclaimer".

Example: a small online shop

A shop run from a phone and a website collects names, addresses and payments, so it needs a privacy policy. It sells goods, so it needs terms of use and a refund policy that say what a buyer may expect. If it reviews products or links to affiliate sellers, it adds a disclaimer. A one-page portfolio with no form needs only a short privacy line. Make the set small, accurate and linked from the footer, and review it whenever the business changes.

Common questions

Can I combine them in one page?

You can, but separate pages are easier to link, update and find. Advertising networks expect a separate privacy policy page.
