Privacy policy for a mobile app

App stores will not list your app without a privacy policy link. This page starts the generator for an app that has sign-in and analytics; change the ticks to match your app.

A generated policy is a starting point, not legal advice. It is a general draft written from your answers. Read it, check it against what your site really does, and ask a lawyer if your case is not simple.

What the stores expect

Both Google Play and the Apple App Store require a link to a privacy policy for apps that collect personal data, and ask you to describe the data in a separate form in their consoles. The policy and the console form must agree. Read the current requirements in the Play Console and App Store Connect help pages before you submit.

Things apps collect that websites do not

  • Device identifiers and crash reports from tools such as Firebase.
  • Location, camera, microphone, contacts or photos, but only if the app asks for the permission. Name each permission in the policy and say why.
  • Push notification tokens.
  • In-app purchase receipts held by the store, not by you.

Writing it for an app

Explain each permission in a sentence a user would understand: "We ask for camera access only to scan a barcode; the image is not saved." Put the policy link inside the app (Settings or About) as well as in the store listing, and keep the web page live as long as the app is installed anywhere.

Use the "other services" box to name the SDKs you ship, for example Firebase, a payment SDK or an ad SDK.

Before you submit the app

List each SDK your app contains. A weather app that includes an ad SDK, a crash reporter and an analytics kit collects data through all three, even if your own code collects nothing. Open the app on a test phone, use it with a network monitor if you can, and compare what leaves the phone with what the policy says.

Update the store forms (Data safety on Google Play, App Privacy on the App Store) at the same time as the policy so the three stay in step.

Common questions

Do I need a policy if my app collects nothing?

Stores still ask for a link. A short policy that says what the app does not collect is accepted and builds trust.

Where do I host the page?

Any public web page works, for example a page on your own site. It must open without a login.
