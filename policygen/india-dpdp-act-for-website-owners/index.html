The DPDP Act and Rules for website owners

This guide is built from the official text of the Act and the Rules, read on 13 November 2026. It explains the duties that matter to a small site in plain words and says which parts start when.

Sources and what we checked

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act 22 of 2023), MeitY, meity.gov.in; the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, Gazette of India Extraordinary notification G.S.R. 846(E) dated 13 November 2025, MeitY, meity.gov.in; and the PIB explainer "DPDP Rules, 2025 Notified" of 17 November 2025, pib.gov.in.

Anything we did not read (the schedules, the rules on significant data fiduciaries, exemptions for start-ups) is not described. Check the official text for your own case.

The main ideas

The Act calls a business that decides why and how personal data is processed a "Data Fiduciary" and the person the data is about a "Data Principal". The Rules say that a notice to the Data Principal must be understandable on its own, in clear and plain language, and must at least give an itemised description of the personal data and the specified purposes, with a description of the goods, services or uses that the processing enables (Rule 3). It must also give the link to your website or app and say how the person can withdraw consent (as easily as it was given), exercise their rights and complain to the Data Protection Board.

When each part starts

Rule 1 of the Rules sets the dates:

  • The Rules were published in the Gazette of India on 13 November 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)).
  • Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 (including the Data Protection Board) came into force on publication.
  • Rule 4 (consent managers) comes into force one year after publication, that is, from 13 November 2026.
  • Rules 3 and 5 to 16, 22 and 23 (notice, security safeguards, breach reporting, children and rights requests among them) come into force eighteen months after publication, which is about 13 May 2027.

What a small site should be doing

These are the duties in the Rules that most affect a small website or app, in our words:

  • Security (Rule 6): protect personal data with reasonable safeguards, including measures such as encryption, masking or virtual tokens, and access control.
  • Breaches (Rule 7): tell each affected person without delay what happened, the likely consequences, what you are doing, what they can do and who to contact; give the Board an initial description without delay and more detail within seventy-two hours.
  • Contact (Rule 9): publish the business contact of a Data Protection Officer, or another person who can answer questions, on your site or app.
  • Rights and grievances (Rule 14): publish how a person can make a request to use their rights, and your grievance system, with a response time not longer than ninety days.
  • Children (Act section 9 and Rule 10): get verifiable consent of a parent or lawful guardian before processing a child's personal data, and do not track or monitor children or target advertising at them. The Act and Rule 12 allow some exemptions that we have not summarised.

Rights of a person, from the Act

The Act gives a person the right to obtain a summary of the personal data being processed and the identities of those it has been shared with (section 11), the right to grievance redressal (section 13) and the right to nominate someone to act if they die or become incapable (section 14). The PIB explainer also lists access, correction, updating, erasure and withdrawal of consent among the rights under the Act and Rules. A person must use your grievance route before approaching the Board (section 13).

Penalties

The PIB explainer of 17 November 2025 states that penalties can reach up to Rs 250 crore for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards, up to Rs 200 crore for failing to report a breach or for violating the duties about children, and up to Rs 50 crore for other violations by a Data Fiduciary. These are maximums set by the Act; the Board decides in each case.

Common questions

Does the Act apply to every website?

It applies to processing of digital personal data in India and, as the Act says, in some cases to processing outside India connected with offering goods or services to people in India. We have not summarised the exemptions; read the Act.

Is a cookie banner required?

The Act and Rules speak of notice and consent for personal data and do not mention cookie banners as such. Whether a cookie identifies a person depends on how it is used. Asking first is the careful course.
