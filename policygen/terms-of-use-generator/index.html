Terms of use generator

Terms of use set the rules for using your site: who may use it, what is allowed, who owns the content and what happens if something goes wrong. Fill in the form and the terms appear below.

A generated policy is a starting point, not legal advice. It is a general draft written from your answers. Read it, check it against what your site really does, and ask a lawyer if your case is not simple.

Optional clauses

What terms of use do

Terms of use are the agreement between you and your visitors. They are not mandatory for a small blog, but they protect you in three ordinary situations: someone copies your whole site, someone posts something unlawful in your comments, or a visitor blames you for a loss that followed from using your information. Without written terms you have only the general law to rely on.

The generator below writes the standard clauses in plain English: who may use the site, acceptable use, ownership of content, your limit of liability, links to other sites, changes and governing law. Two ticks add optional clauses for user-posted content and paid services.

Fields that need a decision

  • Governing law: the state or country whose law should apply, usually where you live or run the business.
  • Minimum age: 13 is the usual floor for sites that collect any information; 18 if you sell things or take payments.
  • User content: tick this if people can post comments, reviews, photos or files. The clause gives you the right to display and, if needed, remove what they post.
  • Paid services: tick this if you take money. The clause points to your refund policy, so make one with our refund policy generator.

Terms are not the same as a privacy policy

Terms describe the rules of the relationship. A privacy policy describes how you handle personal information. Both are linked from the footer, and they refer to each other. Do not copy a large company's terms: they describe services you do not run and promises you cannot keep.

Common questions

Do I need terms and conditions for a blog?

Not by law in most places, but they are worth having if you allow comments, sell anything, or want to forbid copying your content in bulk.

Can I use another site's terms as a base?

Copying their text is copyright infringement and also imports clauses that do not fit you. Use the generated text and change what does not fit.

Which law should I choose?

Normally the law of the place where you live or where your business is registered. Courts there are the ones you can actually attend.
