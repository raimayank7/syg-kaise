California's CCPA in plain words

California has the best known US state privacy law. This guide is built from the California Attorney General's page on the CCPA, read on 13 November 2026.

Source

Source read on 13 November 2026: California Attorney General, "California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)", oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa (page updated 28 August 2026).

Other US states have their own privacy laws, and there is a federal rule on children's data (COPPA). We have not read them for this page, so it does not describe them. Check each state attorney general's website.

Rights of California consumers

The Attorney General lists these rights: to know what personal information a business collects and how it is used and shared; to delete personal information (with exceptions); to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information, including through a global privacy control signal; to non-discrimination for using these rights; and, since the CPRA amendments began on 1 January 2023, to correct inaccurate information and to limit the use and disclosure of sensitive personal information. Consumers also have the right to be told, before or when information is collected, what types are collected and what may be done with them.

  • Right to know (up to twice a year, free of charge).
  • Right to delete.
  • Right to opt out of sale or sharing.
  • Right to correct.
  • Right to limit use of sensitive personal information.
  • Right to non-discrimination.

What "sharing" means

The Attorney General explains that "sharing" refers specifically to sharing for cross-context behavioural advertising: targeting ads at a consumer based on their activity across many websites. A site that uses an ad network to show interest-based ads may therefore be "sharing" in this sense. Check your ad network's settings and its guidance for California users.

Who is covered

The Attorney General's page says the CCPA applies to many businesses, but we did not read the part that sets out which businesses are covered, so we state no thresholds. Read the page's general information section, or ask a lawyer, to learn whether your business is covered.

What a policy should say

  • Pick "United States" or "All three" in the privacy policy generator to add a plain rights paragraph.
  • State whether you sell or share personal information, and how to opt out.
  • Give a contact email for requests and answer them.
  • Do not treat people worse for using their rights.

Common questions

Does the law apply to a site hosted outside the US?

The Attorney General's page speaks of California residents and of businesses subject to the CCPA. Where your server is does not decide it; whether your business is covered does.

Do I need a "Do not sell or share my personal information" link?

Only if your business is covered and sells or shares personal information in the law's sense. Read the Attorney General's section on opting out.
