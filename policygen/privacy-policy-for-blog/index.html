Privacy policy for a blog

Most blogs collect more than their owners think: comment names and emails, newsletter addresses, analytics identifiers and advertising cookies. This page starts the generator with those ticked.

A generated policy is a starting point, not legal advice. It is a general draft written from your answers. Read it, check it against what your site really does, and ask a lawyer if your case is not simple.

What do you collect or use?
Outside services you use

What a blog usually collects

Comments: the name and email a reader types, and often an IP address that the comment system stores. Newsletter: an email address held by your mailing service. Analytics: page views and device details. Ads: cookies from your ad network. Embeds: a YouTube video or a social post loads that company's cookies as soon as the page opens.

The preset ticks all of these. Untick what you do not use. If you moderate comments, add the email service name in the "other services" box.

Mistakes bloggers make

  • Copying a policy from another blog that mentions "our app" or "our online store".
  • Forgetting the ad network: the policy must say that third parties set cookies for advertising.
  • Leaving the policy off the footer of posts, where most readers arrive from search.
  • Saying "we never share data" while a stats script shares it with its provider.

Blogger, WordPress and others

On Blogger, create a Page called Privacy Policy and paste the HTML version. On WordPress, use a Page, not a post. Then add the page to your footer or menu so it shows on every post. See our guide on adding policy pages for the exact steps.

Before you publish a blog policy

Check your theme and plugins for hidden collectors: some comment systems, related-post widgets and font services load from outside servers. Check, too, that the policy link also appears in the footer of single posts, because most readers land on a post and never see the home page.

If you write about health or money, add a disclaimer page next to the policy; our disclaimer generator takes a minute.

Common questions

Do small blogs need a privacy policy?

If you show ads, use analytics, or take comments, yes. Ad networks require it, and readers expect it.

Should the policy mention Disqus or Jetpack comments?

Yes. Any comment service is a third party that handles names and emails. Add it in the other services box.
