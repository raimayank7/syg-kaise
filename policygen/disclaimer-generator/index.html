Disclaimer generator

A disclaimer tells readers what your content is and is not. It matters most for blogs about health, money, law, affiliate reviews and any site that shows ads.

A generated policy is a starting point, not legal advice. It is a general draft written from your answers. Read it, check it against what your site really does, and ask a lawyer if your case is not simple.

Which statements apply?

What a disclaimer can and cannot do

A disclaimer sets expectations: the content is general, may be out of date, is not professional advice and carries no guarantee. It helps if a reader blames you for a decision, and it satisfies the rule in many affiliate and ad programmes that you tell readers about commercial links.

It cannot excuse false claims. If your blog says a supplement cures a disease, a disclaimer will not save you. Write honest content first and use the disclaimer as an addition.

Tick what is true for your site

  • Not professional advice: for law, tax, accounts or any expert subject.
  • Health: for any content about the body, diet, fitness or medicine.
  • Money and investments: for finance, tax and investing content.
  • Affiliate links: if you earn when readers buy through your links. Many networks and the advertising rules of several countries require this to be stated clearly near the link, not only in the footer.
  • Advertising: if the site shows ads.
  • No guaranteed results: for courses, income claims, exam tips or results stories.

Where to place it

Keep a full disclaimer page linked in the footer, and put a one-line version at the top of any page that contains affiliate links or health or money advice. Readers decide within seconds; a link nobody sees protects nobody.

Common questions

Is a disclaimer required by law?

Not as a single document, but several rules require specific disclosures, for example telling readers about paid or affiliate links. A disclaimer page is the easiest way to meet them.

Do I need one if I run AdSense?

AdSense expects a privacy policy and honest content; a disclaimer is not a stated requirement but is expected on advice-type sites. Read Google's current programme policies for the full list.

Can I copy a big site's disclaimer?

No. Their text is copyrighted and describes their business. Use the generated one and edit it.
