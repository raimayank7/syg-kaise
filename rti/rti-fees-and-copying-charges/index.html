RTI fees: what the Central Rules, 2012 say

The Central Rules set the application fee and the charges for supplying information. This page lists each charge as the Rules say it and notes that states differ.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Right to Information Act, 2005 (Gazette of India, Extraordinary, 21 June 2005, the copy kept at rti.gov.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 2 April 2025) and the Right to Information Rules, 2012 (G.S.R. 603(E), 31 July 2012, the copy on cic.gov.in). We read the Act as originally published and the Rules of 2012. Later amendments of the Act (other than section 8(1)(j), see our page on personal information) and any amending rules were not read, so check the current text on the India Code or the Department of Personnel and Training site before you rely on a figure.

Application fee: rule 3

An application under section 6(1) “shall be accompanied by a fee of rupees ten and shall ordinarily not contain more than five hundred words, excluding annexures”. The proviso says no application shall be rejected only on the ground that it contains more than five hundred words.

Charges for supplying information: rule 4

Rule 4 sets the fee for providing information under section 4(4) and section 7(1) and (5) at these rates:

  • (a) rupees two for each page in A-3 or smaller size paper;
  • (b) actual cost or price of a photocopy in large size paper;
  • (c) actual cost or price for samples or models;
  • (d) rupees fifty per diskette or floppy;
  • (e) the price fixed for a publication, or rupees two per page of photocopy for extracts from the publication;
  • (f) no fee for inspection of records for the first hour of inspection and a fee of rupees 5 for each subsequent hour or fraction of an hour; and
  • (g) so much of the postal charge involved in supplying the information as exceeds fifty rupees.

Exemption: rule 5

No fee under rules 3 and 4 is charged from any person who is below the poverty line, if a copy of the certificate issued by the appropriate Government is submitted with the application.

How the officer must ask for further fees: section 7(3)

If the officer decides to provide the information on payment of a further fee, section 7(3) says he or she shall send you an intimation with the details of the fee and the calculation, request you to deposit it, and tell you about your right to seek review of the amount of fee charged or the form of access, including the appellate authority, time limit and process. The days between sending that notice and your payment are not counted in the thirty days.

When the office is late: section 7(6)

Section 7(6) says that where a public authority fails to comply with the time limits in section 7(1), the information shall be provided free of charge, notwithstanding the fee provisions.

States

The Rules we read are the Central Government’s; they apply to a request to a Central Public Information Officer. For a state department or office, the Act says the fee is “as may be prescribed”, and section 2(g) says prescribed means prescribed by rules made by the appropriate Government, so a state may set different fees. We state no state fee: read your state’s own RTI rules.

Common questions

Is the Rs 10 application fee the same in every state?

Not necessarily. The Rs 10 is in the Central Rules. For states, the Act leaves the fee to rules made by the appropriate Government, so look up your state’s rules.

Is an unreasonable fee a ground to complain?

Section 18(1)(d) lists a person who has been required to pay an amount of fee that he or she considers unreasonable among those who can complain to the Information Commission.
