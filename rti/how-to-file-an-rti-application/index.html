How to file an RTI application, step by step

An RTI application is a short written request, with the prescribed fee, sent to the right officer. This page follows section 6 of the Act and rules 3 to 6 of the Central Rules.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Right to Information Act, 2005 (Gazette of India, Extraordinary, 21 June 2005, the copy kept at rti.gov.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 2 April 2025) and the Right to Information Rules, 2012 (G.S.R. 603(E), 31 July 2012, the copy on cic.gov.in). We read the Act as originally published and the Rules of 2012. Later amendments of the Act (other than section 8(1)(j), see our page on personal information) and any amending rules were not read, so check the current text on the India Code or the Department of Personnel and Training site before you rely on a figure.

Step 1: decide which office holds the information

Section 6(1) says the request goes to the Public Information Officer of the concerned public authority, or to the Assistant Public Information Officer. Section 6(3) says that if you apply to an authority that does not hold the information, or whose functions are more closely connected with another authority, it shall transfer the application, or the relevant part, to that other authority and tell you at once, and the transfer must be made as soon as practicable and in no case later than five days from receipt.

Step 2: write the request

Section 6(1) says the request is made in writing or through electronic means, in English, Hindi or the official language of the area, and must specify the particulars of the information sought. If you cannot write it, the proviso says the officer shall render all reasonable assistance to reduce an oral request to writing. Rule 3 of the Central Rules says the application should ordinarily not contain more than five hundred words, excluding annexures, with the address of the officer and of the applicant, and adds that no application shall be rejected only because it is longer.

Step 3: do not give reasons

Section 6(2) says you are not required to give any reason for asking or any personal details except those needed to contact you. Give your name, address and, if you like, a phone number or e-mail.

Step 4: pay the fee

Rule 3 sets an application fee of rupees ten for a request to a Central Public Information Officer. Rule 6 lists three ways of paying: in cash to the public authority or its Assistant Public Information Officer against a proper receipt; by demand draft, bankers cheque or Indian Postal Order payable to the Accounts Officer of the public authority; or by electronic means to the Accounts Officer if the authority can receive fees electronically. The Rules we read are the Central Government’s; they apply to a request to a Central Public Information Officer. For a state department or office, the Act says the fee is “as may be prescribed”, and section 2(g) says prescribed means prescribed by rules made by the appropriate Government, so a state may set different fees. We state no state fee: read your state’s own RTI rules.

Step 5: if you are below the poverty line

Rule 5 says no fee under rules 3 and 4 is charged from a person below the poverty line who submits a copy of the certificate issued by the appropriate Government with the application. Section 7(5)’s proviso says the same for persons below the poverty line as determined by the appropriate Government.

Step 6: keep proof and a date

Keep a copy of the application, the receipt or payment proof and the date the office received it. The time limits on our time-limits page run from receipt.

Common questions

How much is the application fee?

For a request to a Central Public Information Officer, rule 3 of the Central RTI Rules, 2012 says rupees ten. State fees are set by each state and we have not read them.

Can I apply online?

Section 6(1) allows a request “in writing or through electronic means”, and rule 6(c) allows fees by electronic means if the authority has the facility. We did not read any portal’s own page, so check the department’s site.
