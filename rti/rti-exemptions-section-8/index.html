RTI exemptions in section 8, in plain words

An office can refuse only for the reasons the Act lists. This page lists them as they are written and shows the safeguards.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Right to Information Act, 2005 (Gazette of India, Extraordinary, 21 June 2005, the copy kept at rti.gov.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 2 April 2025) and the Right to Information Rules, 2012 (G.S.R. 603(E), 31 July 2012, the copy on cic.gov.in). We read the Act as originally published and the Rules of 2012. Later amendments of the Act (other than section 8(1)(j), see our page on personal information) and any amending rules were not read, so check the current text on the India Code or the Department of Personnel and Training site before you rely on a figure.

The grounds: section 8(1)

Section 8(1) says there is no obligation to give a citizen:

  • (a) information whose disclosure would prejudicially affect the sovereignty and integrity of India, the security, strategic, scientific or economic interests of the State, relations with a foreign State, or lead to incitement of an offence;
  • (b) information expressly forbidden to be published by a court or tribunal, or whose disclosure may constitute contempt of court;
  • (c) information whose disclosure would cause a breach of privilege of Parliament or the State Legislature;
  • (d) commercial confidence, trade secrets or intellectual property whose disclosure would harm the competitive position of a third party, unless the competent authority is satisfied that larger public interest warrants disclosure;
  • (e) information available to a person in his fiduciary relationship, unless the competent authority is satisfied that larger public interest warrants disclosure;
  • (f) information received in confidence from a foreign Government;
  • (g) information whose disclosure would endanger the life or physical safety of any person or identify the source of information or assistance given in confidence for law enforcement or security purposes;
  • (h) information which would impede the process of investigation or apprehension or prosecution of offenders;
  • (i) cabinet papers, with the provisos that the decisions of the Council of Ministers, the reasons and the material on which they were taken shall be made public after the decision is taken and the matter is complete or over, and that matters that come under the exemptions in this section shall not be disclosed;
  • (j) personal information; the wording of this clause was changed by the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, see our separate page.

Public interest can override: section 8(2)

Notwithstanding the Official Secrets Act, 1923 and the exemptions in section 8(1), a public authority may allow access to information if the public interest in disclosure outweighs the harm to the protected interests.

Old records: section 8(3)

Subject to clauses (a), (c) and (i) of section 8(1), information relating to any occurrence, event or matter that took place twenty years before the date of the request shall be provided to anyone who asks. If a question arises about the date from which twenty years is computed, the Central Government’s decision is final, subject to the usual appeals.

Give the part that is not exempt: section 10

Where a request is rejected because the information is exempt, access may be given to the part of the record that has no exempt information and can reasonably be severed, and the officer must tell you that only part is provided and give the reasons.

Third parties: section 9 and section 11

Section 9 says, without prejudice to section 8, that the officer may reject a request where providing access would involve an infringement of copyright subsisting in a person other than the State. Section 7(7) tells the officer to take into account a third party’s representation under section 11 before deciding.

Common questions

Can an office refuse just by saying “exempt”?

Section 7(8) requires the reasons for rejection, the appeal period and the particulars of the appellate authority, and section 19(5) puts the burden of proving that a denial was justified on the officer.

Is there any information every office must give?

The proviso to section 8(1)(j) in the Act as published said information that cannot be denied to Parliament or a State Legislature shall not be denied to any person. That proviso is part of the old clause (j), whose text was later substituted: see our page on personal information.
