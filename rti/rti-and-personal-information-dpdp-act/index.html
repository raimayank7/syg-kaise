RTI section 8(1)(j) and the DPDP Act, 2023

The exemption for personal information in the RTI Act was rewritten by the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. This page shows both wordings.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Right to Information Act, 2005 (Gazette of India, Extraordinary, 21 June 2005, the copy kept at rti.gov.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 2 April 2025) and the Right to Information Rules, 2012 (G.S.R. 603(E), 31 July 2012, the copy on cic.gov.in). We read the Act as originally published and the Rules of 2012. Later amendments of the Act (other than section 8(1)(j), see our page on personal information) and any amending rules were not read, so check the current text on the India Code or the Department of Personnel and Training site before you rely on a figure.

The old clause (j), as published in 2005

“(j) information which relates to personal information the disclosure of which has no relationship to any public activity or interest, or which would cause unwarranted invasion of the privacy of the individual unless the Central Public Information Officer or the State Public Information Officer or the appellate authority, as the case may be, is satisfied that the larger public interest justifies the disclosure of such information: Provided that the information which cannot be denied to the Parliament or a State Legislature shall not be denied to any person.”

What the DPDP Act says it substitutes

Section 44(3) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (copy in the Gazette of India, Extraordinary, read in full) says: “In section 8 of the Right to Information Act, 2005, in sub-section (1), for clause (j), the following clause shall be substituted, namely:— ‘(j) information which relates to personal information;’.” So the substituted clause is shorter: it has no public-interest or privacy qualification, and no proviso.

What the government says about it

The Press Information Bureau explainer on the DPDP Rules, 2025 (pib.gov.in, PRID 2148944, November 2025) says the DPDP Act revises section 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act, that the revision does not prevent the disclosure of personal information but requires it to be assessed with care, and that section 8(2) of the RTI Act, the public interest override, “remains fully operative”. We quote the government’s own description and do not add our own view of how courts or commissions will read the clause.

When it applies: G.S.R. 843(E)

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology notification G.S.R. 843(E), dated New Delhi, 13 November 2025 (read in the Gazette PDF on meity.gov.in; its digital signature is stamped 14 November 2025), says the Central Government appoints the date of publication of the notification in the Official Gazette as the date on which sub-section (2) of section 1, section 2, sections 18 to 26, sections 35, 38 to 43 and sub-sections (1) and (3) of section 44 of the DPDP Act come into force. Sub-section (3) of section 44 is the one that substitutes clause (j) of section 8(1) of the RTI Act, so on the face of that notification the substituted clause has applied since the date of its publication. The same notification gives later dates (one year and eighteen months) to other provisions; none of them concerns the RTI amendment.

We have not read any court order on the amendment, and we say nothing about how it will be applied. Check the current text of section 8(1)(j) on India Code or the Department of Personnel and Training site.

How to write a request that touches a person

Say what record you want and why it is part of official working, such as a file note, an order or a public payment, rather than a private detail of a person. The Act does not require reasons for a request (section 6(2)), but if an officer refuses under clause (j) the first appeal and section 8(2) remain available.

Common questions

Has the old clause been replaced?

Section 44(3) of the DPDP Act substitutes a new clause (j), and notification G.S.R. 843(E), dated 13 November 2025, brings section 44(3) into force from the date of its publication in the Gazette.
