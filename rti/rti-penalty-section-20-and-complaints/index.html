RTI penalties: section 20 and complaints under section 18

Section 20 lets the Information Commission fine an officer for delay or wrongful refusal. This page sets out when, and what section 18 lets you complain about.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Right to Information Act, 2005 (Gazette of India, Extraordinary, 21 June 2005, the copy kept at rti.gov.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 2 April 2025) and the Right to Information Rules, 2012 (G.S.R. 603(E), 31 July 2012, the copy on cic.gov.in). We read the Act as originally published and the Rules of 2012. Later amendments of the Act (other than section 8(1)(j), see our page on personal information) and any amending rules were not read, so check the current text on the India Code or the Department of Personnel and Training site before you rely on a figure.

When a penalty can be imposed: section 20(1)

At the time of deciding a complaint or appeal, if the Commission thinks the officer, without reasonable cause, refused to receive an application, did not furnish information in time, malafidely denied the request, knowingly gave incorrect, incomplete or misleading information, destroyed information that was the subject of the request, or obstructed furnishing the information, it shall impose a penalty of two hundred and fifty rupees each day until the application is received or the information is furnished. The total shall not exceed twenty-five thousand rupees.

The officer’s side

The first proviso says the officer shall be given a reasonable opportunity of being heard before any penalty is imposed. The second proviso says the burden of proving that he or she acted reasonably and diligently is on the officer.

Persistent default: section 20(2)

Where the failure is without reasonable cause and persistent, the Commission shall recommend disciplinary action against the officer under the service rules applicable to him or her.

What you can complain about: section 18(1)

A person can complain to the Information Commission if unable to submit a request because no officer was appointed or the Assistant officer refused to accept the application; if refused access to information; if given no response within the time limit; if asked to pay an amount of fee considered unreasonable; if given incomplete, misleading or false information; or in respect of any other matter relating to requesting or obtaining access to records.

What a penalty does not do

The penalty is paid by the officer under the Commission’s order, not to you. Your remedy is the information itself, and for delay, section 7(6) makes it free.

Common questions

Can I ask the Commission to fine an officer?

Section 20(1) says the Commission imposes the penalty when deciding a complaint or appeal if it forms the opinions listed; you can bring the delay to its notice in your complaint or appeal.
