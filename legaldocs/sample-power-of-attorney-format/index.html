Power of attorney format

A document by which you authorise another person to act for you in stated matters.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

POWER OF ATTORNEY I, [Donor's full name], son/daughter/wife of [Parentage], resident of [Donor's address] (the Donor), appoint [Attorney's full name], son/daughter/wife of [Parentage], resident of [Attorney's address] (the Attorney), as my attorney, to act for me and in my name in the matters listed below, on [Date]. 1. To [State the first thing the Attorney may do, for example collect rent from the property at (address)]. 2. To [State the second thing, for example deal with a named bank account]. 3. To sign, execute and present documents needed for the above matters, in the Attorney's own name or in my name, and to do all acts necessary for them. 4. This power is for [Fixed period or until I revoke it in writing] and does not extend to anything not listed above. 5. I will treat everything the Attorney lawfully does under this power as done by me. DONOR: ______________________ ACCEPTED by the Attorney: ______________________ WITNESS 1: [Witness 1 name and address] Signature: ______________________ WITNESS 2: [Witness 2 name and address] Signature: ______________________

What this sample is for

A document by which you authorise another person to act for you in stated matters.

What the law says (sections read on 13 November 2026)

  • Section 1A of the Powers-of-Attorney Act, 1882: a "Power-of-Attorney" includes any instrument empowering a specified person to act for and in the name of the person executing it.
  • Section 2: the donee may execute an instrument in his own name and signature by the authority of the donor, and it is as effectual as if done in the donor's name.
  • Section 3 protects a person who acts in good faith under a power without knowing that the donor has died, become of unsound mind or insolvent, or revoked the power.
  • Registration Act sections 32(c) and 33: an agent presenting a document for registration must hold a power of attorney recognised under section 33.

Check before you sign

  • List only the powers you want to give. A general power is wide; a narrow one is safer.
  • Tell banks and offices in writing when you revoke a power.
  • Ask the office where the attorney will act what form it accepts and whether the power must be registered or authenticated.

Stamp paper, registration and fees

This site states no stamp duty, registration fee or state rule. They differ by state and change. Ask the Sub-Registrar, the state stamp office or a lawyer. Sections 3 and 35 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 deal with instruments chargeable with duty and with unstamped instruments; see our stamp paper page.

Common questions

Is this sample enough on its own?

No. It is a general sample. Registration, stamp paper, witnesses and local rules depend on the document and your state. Ask the Sub-Registrar or a lawyer before you sign.
