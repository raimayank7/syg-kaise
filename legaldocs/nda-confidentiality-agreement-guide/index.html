NDA (confidentiality agreement): what the Contract Act says

The Indian Contract Act has no section on NDAs as such. A non-disclosure agreement is an ordinary contract, so the general sections decide what it can and cannot do.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Source

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Indian Contract Act, 1872, India Code copy (indiacode.nic.in, file A187209.pdf, 53 pages, read through the Internet Archive snapshot of 5 May 2026 because the live India Code site answered HTTP 504; the snapshot may be older than the current India Code text). Sections 10, 11, 23, 27, 28, 73 and 74 were read in it. An earlier copy hosted by the Commercial Tax Department of Uttar Pradesh had section 28 in its older form. Nothing on this page is a rule for NDAs specifically.

It is a contract if it meets section 10

Section 10 says all agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not expressly declared void. It adds that nothing in the Act affects any law requiring a contract to be in writing or in the presence of witnesses, or any law relating to the registration of documents. Section 11 says every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, is of sound mind and is not disqualified from contracting by any law.

Lawful object: section 23

The consideration or object is lawful unless it is forbidden by law, would defeat the provisions of any law, is fraudulent, involves or implies injury to the person or property of another, or the Court regards it as immoral or opposed to public policy; an agreement with an unlawful object or consideration is void. An NDA cannot be used to hide a crime or to stop someone reporting one.

Do not restrain a trade or profession: section 27

Section 27 says "every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void", with an exception for the seller of the goodwill of a business who agrees with the buyer not to carry on a similar business within specified local limits that the court finds reasonable. Keep an NDA to keeping information confidential. A clause that bars a person from working in the same field may be void to that extent under this section.

Limits on dispute clauses: section 28

The India Code copy of section 28 says every agreement (a) by which a party is restricted absolutely from enforcing his rights under or in respect of any contract by the usual legal proceedings in the ordinary tribunals, or which limits the time within which he may enforce them, or (b) which extinguishes the rights of any party, or discharges any party from liability, under the contract on the expiry of a specified period so as to restrict any party from enforcing his rights, is void to that extent. Exception 1 saves a contract to refer to arbitration disputes that may arise (we read the opening of the exceptions only). So an NDA should not cut short the time in which a party can sue.

If it is broken: sections 73 and 74

Section 73 says the party who suffers by a breach is entitled to compensation for loss or damage that naturally arose in the usual course of things or that the parties knew would likely result, not for remote and indirect loss. Section 74 says where a sum is named in the contract as payable on breach, or there is any other stipulation by way of penalty, the complaining party is entitled to reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount named, whether or not actual loss is proved. A very large agreed sum may therefore not be recovered in full.

Writing a sensible NDA

  • Name the parties and say who may disclose what; define the confidential information narrowly.
  • Say how long the duty lasts and what is not covered (information already public, or received lawfully from someone else).
  • Do not restrict the other person's right to earn a living (section 27).
  • Choose how disputes are handled, keeping section 28 in mind.
  • Stamp duty and signing formalities differ by state and are not stated here.

Common questions

Does an NDA have to be on stamp paper?

The Contract Act sections we read do not say. Stamp duty depends on the state and the document; see our stamp paper page and ask the stamp office or a lawyer.
