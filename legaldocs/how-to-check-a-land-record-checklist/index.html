Checking a land record online: a checklist

State portals differ, but a few habits help everywhere. This checklist is built from what the official portals of Haryana, Punjab, Maharashtra and Gujarat showed on 13 November 2026.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Read on 13 November 2026 in a browser: jamabandi.nic.in (Haryana), jamabandi.punjab.gov.in (Punjab), upbhulekh.gov.in (Uttar Pradesh), bhulekh.mahabhumi.gov.in (Maharashtra) and anyror.gujarat.gov.in (Gujarat). We read home pages and menu links only; we did not run any searches. Other states are not covered because we could not open their portals (Rajasthan's apnakhata.raj.nic.in: connection timed out; Madhya Pradesh's mpbhulekh.gov.in: the browser refused the navigation); Uttar Pradesh's upbhulekh.gov.in loaded on a later try and is covered.

Before you open the portal

  • Find your village, tehsil and district names as written in your documents.
  • Find the survey, gat, khasra or khewat number, or the owner name, as the portal of your state asks. Haryana's portal says it searches by owner name, khewat number and khasra numbers; Maharashtra's asks for district, taluka, village and survey or gat number.
  • Have a phone number ready if the portal sends an OTP, and never share it with a third party.

On the portal

  • Type the official address yourself.
  • Choose the right record type (for example 7/12, 8A or property card in Maharashtra; jamabandi or mutation in Punjab and Haryana).
  • Read the language notice: Maharashtra's Mahabhulekh says it is available only in Marathi.
  • Note the date and any reference number the portal gives you.

After you see the record

  • Compare names, area and numbers with your deed or mutation order.
  • If they differ, ask the Patwari, Talathi or tehsil office; use the portal's grievance option where it has one (Punjab lists Grievance Redressal).
  • For a certified or signed copy, follow the portal's own procedure; Gujarat's page lists procedures for digitally signed copies and Haryana offers a "verifiable copy of nakal" route.

What this checklist does not do

It does not say who owns the land, whether a title is clear, or what fees apply. A lawyer checks title; the registrar and stamp office give fees.

Common questions

Can I check a record without going to the office?

The portals above say they provide records online. Whether an online copy is accepted for your purpose is for the office that needs it.
