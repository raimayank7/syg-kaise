Gift deed format

A deed by which an owner gives property to another person without payment.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

GIFT DEED This deed of gift is made at [Place] on [Date] between: [Donor's full name], son/daughter/wife of [Parentage], resident of [Donor's address] (the Donor); and [Donee's full name], son/daughter/wife of [Parentage], resident of [Donee's address] (the Donee). 1. The Donor is the owner of the property described below (the Property): [Describe the property fully with boundaries and number]. 2. Out of love and affection and not for any money or other payment, the Donor voluntarily gifts the Property to the Donee. 3. The Donee accepts the gift, and the Donee confirms that this acceptance is made during the lifetime of the Donor. 4. The Donor has handed over possession of the Property to the Donee, and the Donor declares that the Property is free from any dispute, to the best of the Donor's knowledge. 5. The Donor has the full right to make this gift, and no part of it comprises property that the Donor does not own today. Signed by the Donor and accepted by the Donee in the presence of the witnesses below. What this sample is for

A deed by which an owner gives property to another person without payment.

What the law says (sections read on 13 November 2026)

  • Section 122 of the Transfer of Property Act defines a gift as a voluntary transfer without consideration of existing property, accepted by or on behalf of the donee during the donor's lifetime.
  • Section 123: a gift of immovable property must be made by a registered instrument signed by or on behalf of the donor and attested by at least two witnesses.
  • Section 124: a gift of future property is void as to the future part. Section 126: a gift revocable at the mere will of the donor is void wholly or in part.
  • Registration Act section 17(1)(a): instruments of gift of immovable property shall be registered.

Check before you sign

  • Do not include property the donor does not own yet (section 124).
  • Do not add a clause saying the gift can be cancelled at the donor's wish (section 126).
  • Register the deed; stamp duty and fees are not stated here.
  • Have a lawyer check title and any state rules.

Stamp paper, registration and fees

This site states no stamp duty, registration fee or state rule. They differ by state and change. Ask the Sub-Registrar, the state stamp office or a lawyer. Sections 3 and 35 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 deal with instruments chargeable with duty and with unstamped instruments; see our stamp paper page.

Common questions

Is this sample enough on its own?

No. It is a general sample. Registration, stamp paper, witnesses and local rules depend on the document and your state. Ask the Sub-Registrar or a lawyer before you sign.
