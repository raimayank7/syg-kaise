Company, LLP and partnership compared

Choosing a business form starts with what each Act says about who can form it, how it is named and what its members are liable for.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Who can form it

  • Public company: seven or more persons (Companies Act s.3(1)(a)).
  • Private company: two or more persons (s.3(1)(b)).
  • One Person Company: one person, which is a private company (s.3(1)(c)); s.2(62) defines it as a company that has only one person as a member.
  • Limited liability partnership: two or more persons associated for carrying on a lawful business with a view to profit subscribe their names to an incorporation document (LLP Act s.11(1)(a)).
  • Partnership: persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (Partnership Act s.4).

Name

  • A public limited company ends its name with "Limited" and a private limited company with "Private Limited" (Companies Act s.4(1)(a)), except a company registered under section 8.
  • An LLP's name ends with "limited liability partnership" or "LLP" (LLP Act s.15(1)).
  • A firm name is the name under which the business is carried on (Partnership Act s.4).

Legal person or not

Under Companies Act s.9, from the date of incorporation the subscribers and later members are "a body corporate by the name contained in the memorandum", with perpetual succession and the power to acquire, hold and dispose of property, to contract and to sue and be sued. LLP Act s.14 says an LLP on registration can sue and be sued, own and dispose of property, have a common seal if it decides to, and do other acts a body corporate may. A partnership firm is a relation between persons (Partnership Act s.4); see our partnership deed guide.

Liability

The Companies Act s.4(1)(d) says the memorandum states the liability of members, limited or unlimited. The pages we read do not set out the full rules of liability for LLP partners or firm partners, so this page does not summarise them. Ask a chartered accountant or lawyer.

What we do not cover

Sole proprietorship has no registration Act among the texts we read, so it is not described. Tax, GST, MSME and licences are separate from incorporation and are not covered here. Fees and online forms are on the MCA site.

Common questions

Which form is best for a small business?

The Acts do not say. It depends on liability, tax, funding and compliance, which this site does not cover. Ask a chartered accountant or lawyer.
