Gift deed: what the law says

A gift of immovable property in India has to follow sections 122 and 123 of the Transfer of Property Act and the registration rule in the Registration Act.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (India Code copy); the Registration Act, 1908 (India Code copy).

What a gift is: section 122

Section 122 says a "gift" is "the transfer of certain existing moveable or immoveable property made voluntarily and without consideration, by one person, called the donor, to another, called the donee, and accepted by or on behalf of the donee". Acceptance "must be made during the lifetime of the donor and while he is still capable of giving". If the donee dies before acceptance, the gift is void.

How it is made: section 123

For immovable property, section 123 says "the transfer must be effected by a registered instrument signed by or on behalf of the donor, and attested by at least two witnesses". For moveable property the transfer may be effected either by a registered instrument signed in the same way or by delivery, which may be made in the same way as goods sold may be delivered.

Registration: Registration Act section 17(1)(a)

Section 17(1)(a) of the Registration Act lists "instruments of gift of immovable property" among documents that "shall be registered". Section 49 says a document required to be registered does not affect the immovable property comprised in it unless registered.

What it means in practice

  • A gift of land or a house needs a written instrument, signed by the donor, with at least two attesting witnesses, and registered.
  • The donee must accept during the donor's lifetime.
  • No payment is made for the gift; if there is a price, it is a sale (section 54 of the same Act), not a gift.

Common questions

Can I gift property by a simple letter?

For immovable property, section 123 says no: a registered instrument signed by the donor and attested by at least two witnesses is required.
