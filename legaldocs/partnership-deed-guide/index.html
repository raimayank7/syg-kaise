Partnership deed: what the law says

A partnership deed records how partners agree to share profits. The Indian Partnership Act, 1932 sets the legal frame.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 (India Code copy).

Definition: section 4

Section 4 says "partnership" is "the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all". The persons are partners, collectively a firm, and the name under which the business is carried on is the firm name.

Contract, not status: section 5

Section 5 says the relation of partnership arises from contract and not from status, and that members of a Hindu undivided family carrying on a family business as such are not partners in that business. Section 6 says that in deciding whether a group is a firm, regard shall be had to the real relation between the parties, as shown by all relevant facts together.

At will or for a term: section 7

Section 7 says that where no provision is made by contract between the partners for the duration of the partnership or for its determination, it is a "partnership at will". Say in the deed whether the partnership is for a fixed term or at will.

Registration of the firm: section 58 and its state versions

Central text, section 58(1): registration of a firm "may be effected at any time" by an application to the Registrar of the area in which any place of business of the firm is situated or proposed to be situated, stating the firm name, the place or principal place of business, other places of business, the date each partner joined, the full names and permanent addresses of the partners, and the duration of the firm. Section 58(4) bars certain words, for example "Reserve Bank" and words implying Government sanction or patronage, from firm names unless the State Government consents.

The India Code copy we read also prints state amendments under section 58, and they differ. An Uttarakhand amendment (Act 5 of 2019) makes the application an online one with prescribed fees and says the statement is to be verified by an affidavit certified by a notary on non-judicial stamp paper of a stated value, with the registration certificate downloadable once the fee is paid online. A Rajasthan amendment (Act 10 of 1971) substitutes provisions on names the State Government finds undesirable and on words that need its sanction. A Maharashtra amendment deletes the words "at any time", adds the nature of the business and a true copy of the deed of partnership to what is sent, and sets a period of one year from the constitution of the firm for sending the statement. We read the opening of the Maharashtra amendment only. Which version applies to you depends on your state, so read section 58 as applicable there and ask a chartered accountant or lawyer. We state no stamp-paper value or fee.

If the firm is not registered: section 69

Section 69 says no suit to enforce a right arising from a contract or conferred by the Act can be brought by or on behalf of a person suing as a partner against the firm or any alleged partner, unless the firm is registered and the person suing is shown in the Register of Firms as a partner; and no suit to enforce a right arising from a contract can be brought by or on behalf of a firm against a third party unless the firm is registered and the persons suing are shown as partners. The section lists exceptions, for example a suit for dissolution or for accounts of a dissolved firm.

What to put in a deed

A deed commonly records the firm name, business, address, capital, profit shares, duties, bank operation, duration and how a partner may leave. The law above only sets the frame; the deed fills in the rest. Stamp duty and fees are not stated here.

Common questions

Is registration of a firm compulsory?

Under the central text of section 58(1), registration "may be effected at any time". Some states have amended the section; for example, the India Code copy shows a Maharashtra amendment that sets a one-year period. Section 69 describes what an unregistered firm cannot do in court. Ask an accountant or lawyer.
