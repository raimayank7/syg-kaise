Registering or depositing a will

Registration of a will is optional. The Registration Act also lets a testator deposit a will with a Registrar.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Registration Act, 1908 (India Code copy).

Optional: sections 18(e) and 23

Section 18(e) lists "wills" among documents that may be registered. Section 23 sets the four-month limit for presenting documents but excludes wills ("no document other than a will shall be accepted for registration unless presented... within four months").

Who may present: section 40

Section 40(1) says "the testator, or after his death any person claiming as executor or otherwise under a will, may present it to any Registrar or Sub-Registrar for registration."

How it is registered: section 41

Section 41(1) says a will presented by the testator "may be registered in the same manner as any other document". Under section 41(2), a will presented by another person entitled to present it shall be registered if the registering officer is satisfied that the will was executed by the testator, that the testator is dead, and that the person presenting it is entitled under section 40.

Deposit: section 42

Section 42 says any testator may, personally or by a duly authorised agent, deposit with any Registrar his will "in a sealed cover superscribed with the name of the testator and that of his agent (if any)" with a statement of its nature. We read only the opening of the section; read the rest and ask the Registrar for the procedure and fees.

What it does not do

Registration or deposit does not replace the rules of signing and attestation in section 63 of the Indian Succession Act. A will that does not follow section 63 is not made valid by being registered.

Common questions

Is a registered will stronger than an unregistered will?

The sections we read do not say. They say only that registration is optional and how a will is presented and deposited. Ask a lawyer about proof.
