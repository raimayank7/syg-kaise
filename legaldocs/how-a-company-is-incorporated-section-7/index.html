How a company is incorporated

Incorporating a company means filing a memorandum, articles and declarations with the Registrar. This page reads the sections that say what.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Companies Act, 2013 (India Code copy "As on the 1st September 2018", with the Amendment Acts of 2015, 2019 and 2020 saved with it; later amendments were not read).

This page describes the Act as in the copies we read: the owner-saved India Code copy and the India Code consolidated PDF (370 pages) downloaded again on 13 November 2026. Every section cited here reads the same in both, and neither prints amendments later than 2020, so later amendments are not covered. Companies law is amended often; check the current text on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs site or India Code before you rely on it. We state no filing steps and almost no fees (only the dated few in the incorporation guide): they are in rules and on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs website (mca.gov.in). We also tried the Internet Archive: its copies of the MCA pages are empty page shells (the fee calculator page printed no text), and the only fee-rule document we found there, archived on 25 April 2025, is a 2019 amendment about the ACTIVE form that does not give the incorporation fees. The SPICe+ FAQ document of the Ministry, read through an Internet Archive copy, gave the few fee facts shown in the dated section below. Check any amount on the MCA site on the day you file.

The memorandum: section 4

Section 4(1) says the memorandum states (a) the name of the company, with "Limited" or "Private Limited" as the last words, (b) the State in which the registered office is to be situated, (c) the objects for which the company is incorporated and matters necessary in furtherance of them, and (d) the liability of members, limited or unlimited. We read these four items; the section has more, including provisions on capital that we did not read.

The articles: section 5

Section 5(1) says the articles "shall contain the regulations for management of the company", with additional matters as prescribed (5(2)). Section 5(3) lets the articles contain entrenchment provisions that make some provisions harder to alter than a special resolution allows; under 5(4) these are made either on formation or by amendment agreed by all members of a private company, or by special resolution in a public company.

What is filed: section 7(1)

Section 7(1) says these documents and information are filed with the Registrar within whose jurisdiction the registered office is proposed to be situated: (a) the memorandum and articles signed by all subscribers; (b) a declaration by an advocate, chartered accountant, cost accountant or company secretary in practice engaged in the formation, and by a person named in the articles as a director, manager or secretary, that the requirements of the Act and rules have been complied with; (c) a declaration from each subscriber and first director that he has not been convicted of an offence in connection with the promotion, formation or management of a company, or found guilty of fraud or misfeasance or breach of duty, during the preceding five years, and that the documents filed are correct and complete; (d) the address for correspondence until the registered office is established; (e) particulars of every subscriber with proof of identity; and (f) particulars of the first directors, including their Director Identification Number. Clause (g) deals with particulars of the interests of persons named as first directors in other firms and companies; we read only its opening.

Effect of registration: section 9

From the date of incorporation in the certificate of incorporation, the subscribers and later members are a body corporate by the name in the memorandum, with perpetual succession and power to acquire, hold and dispose of property, to contract and to sue and be sued.

Registered office: section 12

Section 12(1) says a company shall, within thirty days of its incorporation and at all times after, have a registered office capable of receiving and acknowledging communications and notices. Under 12(2) the company furnishes to the Registrar verification of the registered office within thirty days of incorporation in the prescribed manner. Section 12(3) requires the company's name and registered-office address to be painted or affixed outside every office or place where business is carried on, in legible letters.

Fees and the SPICe+ challan: what the Ministry's own FAQ says (dated)

Source: the Ministry of Corporate Affairs document "FAQs on Incorporation and Allied Matters" (SPICe+ and linked filings, V3, file dated 22 January 2023). The mca.gov.in site was under maintenance on 13 November 2026 and refused a direct download, so we read the copy kept by the Internet Archive on 17 April 2026. Fees may have changed since the document was written; the Ministry's own newer amendments were not found. Check the amount on the MCA site on the day you file.

Question 29 says the fees depend on the authorised share capital of the company and that stamp duty rates may differ for different states. It adds that if a company first reserves the name by filing SPICe+ Part A separately, INR 1000 is charged for name reservation, and that PAN (Rs 66) and TAN (Rs 65) fees become payable.

Question 30 says companies incorporated through SPICe+ with an authorised capital up to INR 15,00,000 "would continue to enjoy 'Zero Filing Fee' concession" and are levied only stamp duty as applicable state to state.

Questions 31 and 32 say no additional fee is payable for opening a bank account through SPICe+, and that PAN and TAN fees are not paid separately: a consolidated challan is generated when SPICe+ (INC-32) is filed, containing the fee for the form, the MoA fee, the AoA fee, PAN, TAN and the URC-1 fee if applicable.

Question 22 says a reserved name can be extended on payment of fees through the web service in SPICe+ Part A: 20 to 40 days for Rs 1,000 (paid before the first twenty days end), 40 to 60 days for Rs 2,000 (paid before forty days end), or 20 to 60 days for Rs 3,000 (paid before the first twenty days end).

We state no amount for companies above INR 15,00,000 authorised capital, no stamp duty and no LLP fee: the document gives them only as depending on capital or state, and we found no readable MCA source for the LLP (FiLLiP) fee.
