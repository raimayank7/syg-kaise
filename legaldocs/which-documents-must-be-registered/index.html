Which documents must be registered

The Registration Act, 1908 separates documents that must be registered from those that may be. Here is what sections 17, 18, 23 and 49 say.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Registration Act, 1908 (India Code copy).

Compulsory registration: section 17(1)

Section 17(1) says the following documents "shall be registered" (subject to the conditions about the district and the date the Act came into force in it):

  • (a) instruments of gift of immovable property;
  • (b) other non-testamentary instruments which purport or operate to create, declare, assign, limit or extinguish, in present or in future, any right, title or interest, vested or contingent, of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards, to or in immovable property;
  • (c) non-testamentary instruments which acknowledge the receipt or payment of any consideration on account of the creation, declaration, assignment, limitation or extinction of any such right, title or interest;
  • (d) leases of immovable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent;
  • (e) non-testamentary instruments transferring or assigning a decree, order or award that creates, declares, assigns, limits or extinguishes such a right, title or interest of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards in immovable property.

Contracts to transfer: section 17(1A)

Documents containing contracts to transfer immovable property for consideration, for the purpose of section 53A of the Transfer of Property Act, "shall be registered" if executed on or after the commencement of the 2001 amending Act, and if not registered, "they shall have no effect for the purposes of the said section 53A".

What is excluded: section 17(2)

Section 17(2) says clauses (b) and (c) do not apply to some documents, among them a composition deed, an instrument relating to shares in a joint stock company, and (clauses vii and viii) a grant of immovable property by Government and an instrument of partition made by a Revenue-Officer. The list also covers decrees and orders of courts (with an exception) and other items; read the full sub-section. Section 17(1) also has a proviso that lets the State Government exempt certain short, low-rent leases by notification.

Optional registration: section 18

Section 18 lists documents that "may be registered", including instruments (other than gifts and wills) creating rights in immovable property of a value less than one hundred rupees, leases for any term not exceeding one year and leases exempted under section 17, instruments (other than wills) creating rights in movable property, and wills.

The time limit and the effect of not registering

Section 23 says that, subject to sections 24 to 26, no document other than a will is accepted for registration unless presented to the proper officer within four months from the date of its execution. Section 49 says a document required by section 17 (or by the Transfer of Property Act) to be registered shall not affect the immovable property comprised in it, confer a power to adopt, or be received as evidence of a transaction affecting the property, unless registered; a proviso allows such an unregistered document to be received as evidence of a contract in a suit for specific performance or of a collateral transaction not required to be effected by a registered instrument.

Common questions

Does a will have to be registered?

No: section 18(e) lists wills among the documents that may be registered, and section 23 exempts wills from the four-month limit. See our will guide.

Where do I find the stamp duty?

This site does not state stamp duty or registration fees. They differ by state. Ask the Sub-Registrar or a lawyer.
