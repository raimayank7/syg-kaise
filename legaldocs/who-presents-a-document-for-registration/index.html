Who presents a document for registration

A registered document is presented to the proper registration office by a particular kind of person. Section 32 says who.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Registration Act, 1908 (India Code copy).

Section 32: who may present

Except in the cases mentioned in sections 31, 88 and 89, every document to be registered, whether registration is compulsory or optional, is presented at the proper registration office (a) by some person executing or claiming under it, or (for a copy of a decree or order) claiming under the decree or order, (b) by the representative or assign of such person, or (c) by the agent of such person, representative or assign, "duly authorised by power-of-attorney executed and authenticated in manner hereinafter mentioned".

Section 32A: photograph and fingerprints

Section 32A says every person presenting a document at the proper registration office under section 32 "shall affix his passport size photograph and fingerprints to the document". Where the document relates to the transfer of ownership of immovable property, the passport-size photograph and fingerprints of each buyer and seller mentioned in the document are also to be affixed.

Section 33: powers of attorney

Section 33(1) begins "For the purposes of section 32, the following powers-of-attorney shall alone be recognized". We read only the opening of the section here, so we do not list the recognised powers. If an agent will present your document, read section 33 in full or ask the Sub-Registrar which power of attorney is recognised.

Before you go

  • Check the proper registration office for the property; the Act has rules on where documents are registered (we did not read them for this page).
  • Remember the four-month rule in section 23 for documents other than wills.
  • Bring the persons or their duly authorised agents as section 32 requires.
  • Ask about fees and stamp duty at the office; this site states none.

Common questions

Can my agent register a document for me?

Section 32(c) allows an agent duly authorised by a power of attorney "executed and authenticated in manner hereinafter mentioned". Read section 33 and ask the office.
