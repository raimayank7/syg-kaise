Who inherits without a will: Hindu Succession Act

When a man governed by the Hindu Succession Act dies without a will, sections 8 to 10 and the Schedule say who inherits. This page reads them.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (India Code copy).

This is a guide to what the Act says, not advice on who gets what in your family. Succession depends on facts and on other laws; ask a lawyer.

Who the Act applies to: section 2

Section 2(1) says the Act applies to a person who is a Hindu by religion in any of its forms or developments (including a Virashaiva, Lingayat or follower of the Brahmo, Prarthana or Arya Samaj), to a Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh by religion, and to any other person who is not a Muslim, Christian, Parsi or Jew by religion, unless it is proved that the person would not have been governed by Hindu law or by any custom or usage as part of that law if the Act had not been passed. The Explanation lists who is treated as a Hindu, Buddhist, Jaina or Sikh by religion; we read it only in part.

The order of heirs: section 8

Section 8 says the property of a male Hindu dying intestate devolves (a) firstly, upon the heirs who are relatives specified in class I of the Schedule; (b) secondly, if there is no heir of class I, upon the heirs specified in class II; (c) thirdly, if there is no heir of either class, upon the agnates; and (d) lastly, if there is no agnate, upon the cognates.

Class I heirs: the Schedule

Class I lists: son; daughter; widow; mother; son of a pre-deceased son; daughter of a pre-deceased son; son of a pre-deceased daughter; daughter of a pre-deceased daughter; widow of a pre-deceased son; and further descendants in the branches of pre-deceased sons and daughters, as listed. Section 9 says those in class I "shall take simultaneously and to the exclusion of all other heirs".

How class I shares are divided: section 10

Rule 1: the widow, or all the widows together, take one share. Rule 2: the surviving sons and daughters and the mother each take one share. Rule 3: the heirs in the branch of each pre-deceased son or daughter take between them one share. Rule 4 says how that share is divided within the branch of a pre-deceased son (his widow or widows together and surviving sons and daughters get equal portions, and the branch of his pre-deceased sons gets the same portion) and within the branch of a pre-deceased daughter (surviving sons and daughters get equal portions).

Class II and beyond

Section 11 says property is divided among the heirs in any one entry of class II equally, and section 9 says entries in class II are preferred in order. Sections 12 and 13 deal with agnates and cognates and the computation of degrees; we read only their titles. Section 15 deals with succession in the case of female Hindus; we read only its title, so we do not describe it.

What this does not tell you

It does not decide ancestral or coparcenary property, property of a Muslim, Christian or Parsi, self-acquired versus family property, or the claims of persons under other laws. A legal heir certificate is issued under state rules that this site did not read.

Common questions

Do daughters inherit under this Act?

Section 10 and the class I list in the Schedule include daughters, who take a share as sons do under Rule 2.

Does this apply if there is a will?

The sections here deal with a person who dies intestate, that is, without a will. See our will guide.
