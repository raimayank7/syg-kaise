LLP registration: what the Act says

A limited liability partnership is formed by filing an incorporation document. Sections 7, 11, 14 and 15 of the LLP Act say what it must contain.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Definition: section 2(1)(n)

A "limited liability partnership" means a partnership formed and registered under the LLP Act.

Incorporation: section 11(1)

Two or more persons associated for carrying on a lawful business with a view to profit subscribe their names to an incorporation document; it is filed with the Registrar of the State in which the registered office is to be situated, in the manner and with the fees prescribed; and a statement in the prescribed form by an advocate, a Company Secretary, a Chartered Accountant or a Cost Accountant engaged in the formation, and by one who subscribed to the document, that the requirements of the Act and rules have been complied with, is filed with it.

What the incorporation document states: section 11(2)

  • the name of the LLP;
  • the proposed business;
  • the address of the registered office;
  • the name and address of each person who is to be a partner on incorporation;
  • the name and address of the persons who are to be designated partners on incorporation;
  • such other information as may be prescribed.

Designated partners: section 7

Every LLP shall have at least two designated partners who are individuals, and at least one of them shall be resident in India. The section explains "resident in India" as a person who has stayed in India for a period of not less than a stated number of days during the preceding year; the copy we read shows the number as amended by the 2021 Act. Read the explanation for the exact days.

Name: section 15

The name of every LLP ends with the words "limited liability partnership" or the acronym "LLP" (15(1)). Section 15(2) bars a name that, in the opinion of the Central Government, is undesirable or identical or too nearly resembles the name of another LLP or a company or a registered trade mark.

Effect of registration: section 14

On registration an LLP, by its name, can sue and be sued, acquire, own, hold, develop or dispose of property, have a common seal if it decides to, and do and suffer such other acts as bodies corporate may lawfully do.

Common questions
