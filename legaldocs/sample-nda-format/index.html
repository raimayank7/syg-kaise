Non-disclosure agreement format

A short agreement by which two parties agree to keep each other's confidential information secret.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT This agreement is made at [Place] on [Date] between: [First party's name], [Address] (the First Party); and [Second party's name], [Address] (the Second Party). 1. Purpose. The parties wish to discuss [Describe the purpose, for example a possible business arrangement] and may share confidential information for that purpose only. 2. Confidential information means information that one party gives the other, in writing, orally or in any form, about [Describe the kind of information], and that is marked or would reasonably be understood to be confidential. 3. Each party will keep the other's confidential information secret, use it only for the purpose, and share it only with people who need it for the purpose and who are bound to keep it secret. 4. This duty does not cover information that is or becomes public without a breach of this agreement, was already known to the receiving party, was received lawfully from someone else, or must be disclosed by law or by an order of a court (in which case the receiving party will tell the other party first, where the law allows). 5. On request, each party will return or destroy the other's confidential information. 6. The duty of confidence lasts for [Period] from the date of this agreement. 7. Nothing in this agreement stops either party from carrying on its own lawful trade or business. 8. If a party breaks this agreement, the other party may claim reasonable compensation for the loss caused. [Optional: the parties agree a reasonable sum of Rs (Amount) as compensation for a breach.] 9. Disputes will be [How disputes will be settled]. The law of India applies. FIRST PARTY: ______________________ SECOND PARTY: ______________________ WITNESS 1: [Witness 1 name and address] Signature: ______________________ WITNESS 2: [Witness 2 name and address] Signature: ______________________

What this sample is for

A short agreement by which two parties agree to keep each other's confidential information secret.

What the law says (sections read on 13 November 2026)

  • Section 10 of the Indian Contract Act: agreements are contracts if made by free consent of competent parties, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and not expressly declared void; section 11 says who is competent.
  • Section 23: an agreement with an unlawful object is void; section 27: an agreement that restrains a lawful profession, trade or business is void to that extent (clause 7 of the sample is written with this in mind).
  • Section 73: compensation for loss that naturally arises from a breach; section 74: where a sum is named as payable on breach, reasonable compensation not exceeding that amount.
  • The Act sections we read do not require witnesses or stamp paper for such a contract; section 10 keeps any other law that requires writing, witnesses or registration, and state stamp laws may apply.

Check before you sign

  • Describe the confidential information narrowly and honestly.
  • Do not add a clause that stops someone earning a living in their field (section 27).
  • If you add a fixed sum for breach, keep it reasonable (section 74).
  • Ask a lawyer about stamp duty in your state and about secrets that matter a great deal.

Stamp paper, registration and fees

This site states no stamp duty, registration fee or state rule. They differ by state and change. Ask the Sub-Registrar, the state stamp office or a lawyer. Sections 3 and 35 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 deal with instruments chargeable with duty and with unstamped instruments; see our stamp paper page.

Common questions

Is this sample enough on its own?

No. It is a general sample. Registration, stamp paper, witnesses and local rules depend on the document and your state. Ask the Sub-Registrar or a lawyer before you sign.
