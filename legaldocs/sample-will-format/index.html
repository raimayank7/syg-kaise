Simple will format

A simple will for a person with ordinary property who wants to name who receives what.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

LAST WILL AND TESTAMENT I, [Full name], son/daughter/wife of [Father's or husband's name], aged [Age] years, resident of [Full address], being of sound mind and in good health, make this will of my own free will, without pressure from anyone, on [Date]. 1. I revoke all earlier wills and codicils made by me. 2. I appoint [Name of executor], resident of [Address of executor], as the executor of this will. 3. I give [Describe the property exactly] to [Name of beneficiary], [Relationship], resident of [Address of beneficiary]. 4. I give [Describe the second item] to [Name of second beneficiary]. 5. I give the rest of my property of every kind to [Name of residuary beneficiary]. 6. If any beneficiary dies before me, [State what should happen]. Signed by the testator at [Place] on [Date], in the presence of the witnesses named below. TESTATOR: ______________________ We, the undersigned, each saw the testator sign this will (or saw another person sign it in the testator's presence and by his or her direction, or received from the testator a personal acknowledgment of the signature), and each of us signed in the testator's presence. WITNESS 1: [Name of witness 1], [Address of witness 1] Signature: ______________________ WITNESS 2: [Name of witness 2], [Address of witness 2] Signature: ______________________

What this sample is for

A simple will for a person with ordinary property who wants to name who receives what.

What the law says (sections read on 13 November 2026)

  • Section 59 of the Indian Succession Act says every person of sound mind, not being a minor, may dispose of his property by will.
  • Section 63 requires the testator's signature or mark (or signature by another in the testator's presence and by direction), placed so as to show it gives effect to the writing as a will, and attestation by two or more witnesses, each signing in the testator's presence.
  • Section 61 says a will caused by fraud, coercion or importunity that takes away free agency is void.
  • Registration is optional (Registration Act section 18(e)).
  • Sections 67 and 68: a beneficiary may witness, but the gift to an attesting witness or the witness's spouse is void; so use witnesses who gain nothing.
  • Section 70: a will is revoked only by marriage, by another will or codicil, by a writing declaring an intention to revoke executed like a will, or by destroying it with that intention; section 71: later alterations have no effect unless executed like the will.

Check before you sign

  • Describe property exactly: survey or plot number, account number, address.
  • Sign only when both witnesses are with you.
  • Keep the original safe and tell the executor where it is.
  • Ask a lawyer if you have complex property, minor beneficiaries or family disputes.

Stamp paper, registration and fees

This site states no stamp duty, registration fee or state rule. They differ by state and change. Ask the Sub-Registrar, the state stamp office or a lawyer. Sections 3 and 35 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 deal with instruments chargeable with duty and with unstamped instruments; see our stamp paper page.
