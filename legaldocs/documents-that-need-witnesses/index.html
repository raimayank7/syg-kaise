Which documents need witnesses

Witnesses (attesters) matter for some documents and not for others. Here is what the Acts we read say.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Indian Succession Act, 1925 (India Code copy); the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (India Code copy).

A will: Indian Succession Act section 63(c)

A will shall be attested by two or more witnesses, each of whom has seen the testator sign or affix a mark, or has seen another person sign in the testator's presence and by direction, or has received from the testator a personal acknowledgment of the signature or mark; each witness signs in the presence of the testator; and it is not necessary that more than one witness be present at the same time.

A gift of immovable property: Transfer of Property Act section 123

The transfer must be effected by a registered instrument signed by or on behalf of the donor and "attested by at least two witnesses".

A registered lease: Transfer of Property Act section 107

Section 107 says that where a lease is made by a registered instrument, the instrument must be executed by both the lessor and the lessee. The section we read does not require witnesses for a lease; the registration office may have its own requirements, so ask.

Documents we did not find a witness rule for

In the sections read for this site, we found no witness requirement for a sale instrument under section 54 or a power of attorney under the Powers-of-Attorney Act. Practices differ, and registration offices may ask for identifying witnesses. Ask the Sub-Registrar.

Choosing witnesses

  • Use adults who know you and will be available if questions arise later.
  • Write their full names, parentage and addresses.
  • Have each witness sign in your presence (for a will, the testator's presence is the rule in section 63(c)).
  • Do not use the notary as a substitute for witnesses unless the office confirms it is enough.

Common questions

Can my family members be witnesses?

The sections we read do not say. Ask a lawyer, especially for a will where a witness may also gain under it.
