Punjab land records (jamabandi): what the portal offers

Punjab publishes land records on its Punjab Land Records website, branded Easy Jamabandi. This page lists what the portal's home page offers.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Source

Source: the official Punjab Land Records portal (Easy Jamabandi) page at jamabandi.punjab.gov.in, opened in a browser on 13 November 2026. We read the home page text and its menu links only; we did not search any record. Portals change their menus, so confirm on the portal itself.

What the home page lists

The page is titled "Punjab Land Records" in Punjabi and English and shows "Easy Jamabandi". Its options, in English as shown, are:

  • Official Login
  • Track Application
  • Grievance Redressal
  • View Jamabandi
  • View Mutation
  • View Roznamcha
  • View Registered Deed
  • Fard Request Status
  • View MGMN Final Map-2 Data and Final Map-3 Data
  • Integrated Property
  • FAQ and User Manual
  • Online Services

Items marked as new

Under a "NEW" marker the page lists: a Chief Minister's scheme, "30 days partition, Oral/Family Partition"; "Jamabandi on WhatsApp"; "Mutation of Inheritance"; "Mutation on the basis of Deed"; "Fard badar"; and "Rapat". We did not open these pages and do not explain their rules or time limits.

Who runs it

The page footer reads "Department of Revenue, Rehabilitation and Disaster Management, Government of Punjab, India" and shows a last-updated date of 12/11/2026. The FAQ page shows only the menu to a text reader, but it embeds a PDF of questions and answers, which we read (see the section below). The User Manual page was not opened.

Reading the terms

The portal's own terms are the ones above. For the meaning of jamabandi and mutation, see the Haryana page on this site, which quotes the short descriptions on Haryana's portal; practice and forms can differ by state, so use the Punjab portal's FAQ and User Manual for Punjab.

How to use it carefully

  • Open the portal by typing its address, not through an advertisement.
  • Use "Track Application" with the number you were given, and the grievance option if something is wrong.
  • Ask the Patwari or the Tehsil office if the online record differs from what you hold.
  • We state no fees. The portal and the office give them.

The portal's own FAQ (read 13 November 2026)

The FAQ page is a bilingual document of 14 questions embedded in the page. We read its English text. The fees and time limits below are the portal's own words on the day we read it; the portal can change them, so check on the portal.

Looking at a record (questions 1 and 2): ownership details are in the jamabandi of the village where the land lies. The portal says you can view the online fard (nakal of jamabandi) at easyjamabandi.punjab.gov.in by choosing tehsil and village and giving a khewat number, khasra number or owner name. That copy "is for viewing / your information only". For a legally valid copy the FAQ names two routes: a digitally signed QR-coded fard after paying the requisite fee on the portal, or a fard from a Sewa Kendra or Fard Kendra delivery counter after paying the fee. It says copies from either route are legally valid.

Question 3 explains that a QR-coded fard may show a question mark beside the digital signature ("Validity unknown") and that this does not make it invalid; it gives steps in Adobe Acrobat Reader to add the signer's certificate to trusted certificates and validate the signature until a green tick appears.

Question 4: the service at your doorstep is requested by dialling 1076 and a "Sewa sahayak" visits. The fee figure is cut off in the text we extracted, so we do not state it.

Question 5: a person can subscribe to a khewat for one year on paying a prescribed nominal fee, and updates are then sent by WhatsApp message or email. Question 6: an objection about fraud can be registered through the link on the portal.

Mutation after the owner's death (questions 7 to 10): the request is filed online under the tab "Request on mutation of Inheritance". The form asks for the name of the deceased and death certificate number; the system fetches the certificate from e-Sewa or you upload it. If the deceased left a will, registered or not, you need a copy of the last will, which must not have been cancelled; a registered will can be fetched from the NGDRS portal. The FAQ lists the natural heirs to be entered: for a male, his mother, wife and all children, with the families of predeceased sons or daughters; for a female, her husband and all children, with the children of a predeceased son or daughter. It states a one-time mutation fee of Rs 600 and a decision within 30 days, with WhatsApp messages on the registered mobile number for clarifications or objections.

Other requests (questions 11 to 14): grievances, including a complaint about a demand for a bribe, are filed through the portal tab. Deeds registered on the NGDRS portal are sent automatically to the Patwari for mutation; for an older deed you submit an online request with a copy of the deed, for a fee of Rs 600 and a 30-day timeline, as the FAQ states. Entries of court orders and bank loans: you submit the request with a copy of the order or the bank's documents; no fee is stated for court orders, a bank-loan rapat is entered in 7 days and a court-order rapat in 1 day. Correction of clerical errors: submitted online with a short description and the current fard, decided in 15 days, with no fee.

These are the portal's statements, not our advice, and we have not checked them against the Punjab land revenue law.

Common questions

Can I see a jamabandi online in Punjab?

The portal lists "View Jamabandi" as an option on its home page. How to search, and what is shown, is on the portal and its User Manual.
