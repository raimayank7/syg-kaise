Lease and rent agreement: what the law says

A rent agreement is a lease in the law. The Transfer of Property Act says how a lease is made; the Registration Act says when it must be registered.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (India Code copy); the Registration Act, 1908 (India Code copy).

What a lease is: section 105

Section 105 says a lease of immovable property is "a transfer of a right to enjoy such property, made for a certain time, express or implied, or in perpetuity, in consideration of a price paid or promised, or of money, a share of crops, service or any other thing of value, to be rendered periodically or on specified occasions". The transferor is the lessor, the transferee the lessee, the price the premium, and what is rendered periodically the rent.

How a lease is made: section 107

Section 107 says a lease from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent, "can be made only by a registered instrument". All other leases may be made either by a registered instrument or by oral agreement accompanied by delivery of possession. Where a lease is made by a registered instrument, the instrument (or each of them) must be executed by both the lessor and the lessee. A proviso lets the State Government direct by notification that some leases may be made by unregistered instrument or oral agreement.

Registration Act

Section 17(1)(d) of the Registration Act lists "leases of immovable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent" among documents that shall be registered, with a proviso allowing the State Government to exempt certain short leases of low rent. Section 18(c) lists leases for any term not exceeding one year, and leases exempted under section 17, among documents that may be registered.

Drafting points

  • Name both parties and describe the premises exactly.
  • State the term and the rent; check against section 107 whether it reserves a yearly rent or runs beyond one year.
  • Both lessor and lessee sign a registered instrument.
  • Do not copy the length of term from a neighbour; the rule turns on the facts of your lease and the notifications of your state.
  • Stamp duty and registration fees are not stated here.

Common questions

Is a one-year rent agreement exempt from registration?

Section 107 and section 17(1)(d) turn on whether the lease is from year to year, runs for more than one year, or reserves a yearly rent. Read the sections and ask the Sub-Registrar; your state may also have notifications.
