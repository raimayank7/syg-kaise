Power of attorney: what the law says

A power of attorney lets one person act for another. The short Powers-of-Attorney Act, 1882 sets a few rules.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Powers-of-Attorney Act, 1882 (India Code copy); the Registration Act, 1908 (India Code copy).

Definition: section 1A

Section 1A says "Power-of-Attorney" includes any instrument empowering a specified person to act for and in the name of the person executing it.

Acting in his own name: section 2

Section 2 says the donee of a power of attorney may, if he thinks fit, execute or do any instrument or thing in his own name and signature, and his own seal where sealing is required, by the authority of the donor; and every such instrument and thing "shall be as effectual in law as if it had been executed or done by the donee of the power in the name, and with the signature and seal, of the donor thereof".

Good-faith payments: section 3

Section 3 protects a person who makes a payment or does an act in good faith in pursuance of a power of attorney, even if before that the donor had died, become of unsound mind, become insolvent, or revoked the power, provided the person did not know of the fact at the time.

Deposit with a court: section 4

Section 4(a) says an instrument creating a power of attorney, its execution being verified by affidavit, statutory declaration or other sufficient evidence, "may" be deposited in the High Court or District Court within whose local limits the instrument is. A certified copy of a deposited instrument is sufficient evidence of its contents and of the deposit (section 4(d)). Fees are set by High Court rules (section 4(e)); none is stated here.

Married women: section 5

Section 5 says a married woman of full age has power, as if she were unmarried, by a non-testamentary instrument, to appoint an attorney for executing any non-testamentary instrument or doing any other act which she might herself execute or do.

Presenting documents for registration

Section 32(c) of the Registration Act lets an agent duly authorised by a power of attorney present a document for registration, subject to section 33. See our registration page.

Common questions

Does the Act say a power of attorney must be registered?

The Act itself does not. Section 4 lets a power of attorney be deposited in court. For use before a registration office, read sections 32 and 33 of the Registration Act and ask the Sub-Registrar.
