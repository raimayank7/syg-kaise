Land record terms from the official portals

Land records use words that differ by state. This page lists the terms the official portals use and what their pages say about them.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Source: the official Haryana jamabandi portal page at jamabandi.nic.in, opened in a browser on 13 November 2026. We read the home page text and its menu links only; we did not search any record. Portals change their menus, so confirm on the portal itself.

Also read on the same date: the Punjab Land Records home page (jamabandi.punjab.gov.in), the Mahabhulekh home page (bhulekh.mahabhumi.gov.in) and the Gujarat Any RoR home page (anyror.gujarat.gov.in).

Jamabandi

Haryana's portal defines it as a document prepared as part of the record-of-right in every revenue estate, containing entries about ownership, cultivation and up-to-date of various rights in land, revised every five years when prepared by the Patwari and attested by a Revenue Officer, in two copies. Punjab's portal lists "View Jamabandi" and "Easy Jamabandi".

Mutation (inteqal)

Haryana's portal says mutation indicates the changes to be brought about in ownership and title of land. Punjab's portal lists "View Mutation", "Mutation of Inheritance" and "Mutation on the basis of Deed".

Khewat and khasra

Haryana's portal says land can be searched using the owner name, khewat number and khasra numbers. It also has query links for owner details, kashtkar details, makbuja details, total land and irrigation details. We did not read portal definitions of khewat, khasra, kashtkar or makbuja, so we give none.

7/12, 8A, Property Card and K-Prat (Maharashtra)

Mahabhulekh lists these four as the "Record of Right" types you can select: 7/12, 8A, Property Card and K-Prat. The page does not explain them on its home page, so we do not define them here.

Nakal, roznamcha, fard, rapat

Haryana's portal uses "nakal" for the jamabandi copy it shows and for a verifiable copy. Punjab's portal lists "View Roznamcha", "Fard Request Status", "Fard badar" and "Rapat". We did not open these pages and give no definitions.

Why this page is short on definitions

Meanings can differ by state and by the law of the state. We give a definition only where an official portal gave one on the pages we read. For the rest, use the portal's knowledge base, FAQ or User Manual, or ask the Patwari or tehsil office.

Common questions

Is a jamabandi the same as a title deed?

Haryana's portal calls the jamabandi a record-of-right. The portal pages we read do not call it a title deed, and we do not either. Ask a lawyer about title.
