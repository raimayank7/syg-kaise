Where to file a consumer complaint: sections 34, 35 and 47

A consumer complaint goes first to a District Commission. This page follows sections 34, 35 and 47 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Act 35 of 2019, Gazette of India, Extraordinary; the copy kept at consumeraffairs.nic.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 5 June 2025). We read the Act, the Consumer Protection (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions) Rules, 2020 (G.S.R. 448(E), the copy kept at consumeraffairs.nic.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 5 June 2025) and a Press Information Bureau release of 30 December 2021 on the money limits. We did not read the Jurisdiction Rules, 2021 themselves, any amendment after those dates, or any State rules. Fees and limits change, so check the Commission’s own notice or portal before you file.

Who can file: section 35(1)

A complaint about goods sold or delivered or agreed to be sold or delivered, or a service provided or agreed to be provided, may be filed with a District Commission by (a) the consumer to whom the goods or service were sold, delivered or provided or agreed to be, or who alleges an unfair trade practice in respect of them; (b) any recognised consumer association, whether or not the consumer is a member; (c) one or more consumers, with the District Commission’s permission, on behalf of all consumers with the same interest where there are numerous such consumers; or (d) the Central Government, the Central Authority or the State Government. The proviso says a complaint may be filed electronically in the prescribed manner.

Which District Commission: section 34(2)

A complaint is filed in the District Commission within whose local limits (a) the opposite party, or each of them, ordinarily resides, carries on business, has a branch office or personally works for gain; or (b) any of several opposite parties actually and voluntarily resides, carries on business, has a branch office or personally works for gain, provided the District Commission gives permission; or (c) the cause of action arises wholly or in part; or (d) the complainant resides or personally works for gain. So you can usually file where you live.

The money limit: the Act and the 2021 change

As printed in the Act, a District Commission can entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed one crore rupees, and the State Commission, under section 47(1)(a)(i), complaints where it exceeds one crore but does not exceed ten crore rupees; both provisos say the Central Government may prescribe another value. A Press Information Bureau release of 30 December 2021 (Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution) says the Central Government notified the Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021, and that the revised limits are: up to Rs 50 lakh for District Commissions, more than Rs 50 lakh up to Rs 2 crore for State Commissions, and more than Rs 2 crore for the National Commission. We read the release, not the Rules themselves, so confirm the current limits with the Commission.

Fee

Section 35(2) says every complaint shall be accompanied with the prescribed fee. Rule 7 of the Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions Rules, 2020 sets it; see our page on the fee table.

Appeals go upward

The State Commission also hears appeals against orders of any District Commission within the State (section 47(1)(a)(iii)). See our page on appeals and the time limits.

Online filing: the portal has moved

The page of the filing site edaakhil.nic.in, as archived on 4 August 2025, said only “We’ve moved to e-Jagriti”. The e-Jagriti site (e-jagriti.gov.in) opened for us but its text did not load in our reader, so we cannot describe its screens or any fee. Open it yourself and follow its own instructions.

Common questions

Can I file in the city where I live?

Section 34(2)(d) lets the complaint be instituted in the District Commission within whose local limits the complainant resides or personally works for gain.

Is it e-Daakhil or e-Jagriti?

The archived e-Daakhil page of 4 August 2025 said it had moved to e-Jagriti. Check which one the Department of Consumer Affairs shows today.
