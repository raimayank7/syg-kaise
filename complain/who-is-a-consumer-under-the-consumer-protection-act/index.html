Who is a consumer under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Only a consumer can file a consumer complaint. This page follows section 2(7) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the definition of deficiency in section 2(11).

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Buyers of goods

Section 2(7)(i) says a consumer is a person who buys any goods for a consideration which has been paid or promised or partly paid and partly promised, or under any system of deferred payment. It includes any user of such goods other than the buyer, when the use is made with the approval of the buyer. It does not include a person who obtains the goods for resale or for any commercial purpose.

People who hire or use a service

Section 2(7)(ii) says a consumer is a person who hires or avails of any service for a consideration paid, promised or partly paid and partly promised, or under any system of deferred payment. It includes any beneficiary of the service, other than the person who hired it, when the service is availed of with the approval of the first person. It does not include a person who avails of a service for any commercial purpose.

Livelihood is not “commercial”

The Explanation to section 2(7) says the expression commercial purpose does not include use by a person of goods bought and used by him exclusively for the purpose of earning his livelihood by means of self-employment. It adds that “buys any goods” and “hires or avails any services” include offline or online transactions through electronic means or by teleshopping or direct selling or multi-level marketing.

What a deficiency is: section 2(11)

Section 2(11) says deficiency means any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in the quality, nature and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law, or which has been undertaken to be performed by a person under a contract or otherwise in relation to any service. It includes any act of negligence or omission or commission by such person which causes loss or injury to the consumer, and deliberate withholding of relevant information by such person to the consumer.

Where this takes you next

If you are a consumer and something went wrong, read our page on where to file a complaint, then the steps and the time limit. A complaint letter to the seller or service provider first is a sensible step and our sample follows it.

Common questions

Does a free service count?

Section 2(7) speaks of a consideration paid, promised or partly paid, so a person who pays nothing does not fit the words we read. A lawyer can advise on a particular case.

Does a small shop owner buying goods for the shop count?

Section 2(7)(i) leaves out goods obtained for resale or for any commercial purpose, with the livelihood explanation above. Whether your purchase fits is for a lawyer or the Commission.
