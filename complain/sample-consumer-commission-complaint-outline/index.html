Complaint outline for a District Commission (sample)

A draft of the facts and the relief you ask for. The Commission’s own forms were not read here, so use this only to organise your facts.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

BEFORE THE DISTRICT CONSUMER DISPUTES REDRESSAL COMMISSION, [Name of the district] Complaint under section 35 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 Complainant: [Your full name, address, phone or e-mail] Opposite party: [Name and address of the seller, company or service provider] 1. The complainant is a consumer: [One line: what you bought or hired, when, and for what price, paid by whom]. 2. Jurisdiction: [State why this Commission has it, for example: The opposite party carries on business at [Place]. / The cause of action arose at [Place]. / The complainant resides at [Place].] Value of the goods or services paid as consideration: Rs [Amount]. 3. Facts: [Number each fact in order with the date: purchase, problem, each complaint made and each reply]. 4. Deficiency or defect: [State what is defective or deficient]. 5. Time: The cause of action arose on [Date], so this complaint is within two years under section 69. [If late: ask for the delay to be condoned and give the sufficient cause.] 6. Relief asked: the complainant prays that the Commission direct the opposite party to [Replace the goods / Refund Rs Amount with interest at [Rate] from [Date] / Remove the deficiency] and to pay Rs [Amount] as compensation and Rs [Amount] as costs. 7. Documents: [List each document: bill, correspondence, photographs, warranty card]. Place: [Place] Date: [Date] Complainant’s signature: ______________________ [Your full name] [Add the affidavit, verification and fee as the Commission’s rules require.]

What this sample is for

A draft of the facts and the relief you ask for. The Commission’s own forms were not read here, so use this only to organise your facts.

Which parts of the Act it follows

  • Section 35(1): who can file; the proviso allows electronic filing in the prescribed manner; section 35(2) with rule 7 of the 2020 Rules: the fee, nil up to Rs 5 lakh in the table we read.
  • Section 34(2): the District Commission where the opposite party resides or carries on business, the cause of action arises, or the complainant resides or works for gain.
  • Section 34(1) and the 2021 revision: District Commissions up to Rs 50 lakh per the Press Information Bureau release of 30 December 2021; check the current value.
  • Section 69: two years from the cause of action; section 39(1): the reliefs.

Check before you send

  • This is not the Commission’s official form. Check the Commission’s notices or the filing portal for its form, fee and documents.
  • List every claim with how you worked it out.
  • Attach only true, complete copies.
  • Ask a lawyer or a consumer association if the amount is large.

Sources

Read on 13 November 2026: the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Act 35 of 2019, Gazette of India, Extraordinary; the copy kept at consumeraffairs.nic.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 5 June 2025). We read the Act, the Consumer Protection (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions) Rules, 2020 (G.S.R. 448(E), the copy kept at consumeraffairs.nic.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 5 June 2025) and a Press Information Bureau release of 30 December 2021 on the money limits. We did not read the Jurisdiction Rules, 2021 themselves, any amendment after those dates, or any State rules. Fees and limits change, so check the Commission’s own notice or portal before you file.
