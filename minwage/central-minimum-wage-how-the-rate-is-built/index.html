How the rate is built

What the Labour Bureau’s order says about how the rate is made up: basic wage, VDA, categories and areas.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 14 November 2026: the Labour Bureau order File No. 6/1/2026-VDA dated 30 September 2026 on the revised Variable Dearness Allowance with effect from 1 October 2026 (the Gazette of India Extraordinary PDF linked from labourbureau.gov.in, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 29 October 2026). These are the Central Government’s rates as printed in that order, which is a scan; we read the figures from the page images and checked that basic plus variable dearness allowance equals the printed total in every row. They are not any State’s rates: a State fixes its own minimum wages for the employments under its control. We read fourteen State and Union Territory notices, those of Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Bihar, Telangana, Meghalaya, Odisha, Himachal Pradesh (two employments only), Puducherry (two employments only) and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, which have their own pages; no other State. Rates are revised from time to time; check the Labour Bureau’s latest order.

Basic wage plus variable dearness allowance

The order is titled as an order on the revised rates of Variable Dearness Allowance. It says the Labour Bureau, exercising powers conferred by the Central Government’s notification S.O. 2451(E) dated 12 May 2026 of the Ministry of Labour and Employment, revises the VDA with effect from 1 October 2026. For each employment it prints the VDA rates and then, in a second table, the minimum rates of wages showing the basic rates and the VDA, added up to a total per day.

Why the VDA changed

The order says the revision is on the basis of the average Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers reaching 431.42 from 424.80 as on 30 June 2026 (linked to Base 2016=100), an increase of 6.62 points. It adds that the VDA has been rounded off to the next higher rupee as per the decision of the Minimum Wages Advisory Board.

The law it is issued under

The order states that the instant notification is being issued in pursuance of sub-section (2) of section 69 of the Code on Wages, 2019 (29 of 2019). We did not read the Code.

The four categories

In agriculture, construction and mines the order uses four categories of worker: unskilled, semi-skilled or unskilled supervisory, skilled or clerical, and highly skilled. It says the classification of workers under the different categories is the same as in the Central Government’s earlier notifications cited on each page, which we did not read, so we cannot tell you which job falls in which category.

Areas A, B and C

Some employments are rated by area. The order’s Annexure “Classification of Areas” puts in Area A: Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Delhi, Greater Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Hyderabad, Secunderabad, Kanpur, Chennai, Kolkata, Lucknow, Pune, Nagpur, Faridabad complex, Ghaziabad, Noida and Gurgaon. Area B is a long list of cities (for example Agra, Ajmer, Jaipur, Ludhiana and Patna appear in it), and Note 1 says Area C comprises all areas not mentioned in the list but to which the Minimum Wages Act, 1948 applies. Note 2 says “UA” means urban agglomeration. Read the Annexure for any other city.

Mines: above and below ground

For the mines listed in the order, the rate depends on whether the work is above ground or below ground, not on area.

What the order does not give

The order gives daily rates only; it states no monthly figure, no overtime rate and no State rate. Do not convert it to a month unless the employer’s law or contract says how.
