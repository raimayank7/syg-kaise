About Gandhi Jayanti

Gandhi Jayanti is observed in India on 2 October. The United Nations observes the same day as the International Day of Non-Violence; its page says the day is Mahatma Gandhi's birthday and was established by General Assembly resolution A/RES/61/271 of 15 June 2007.

It is on the same calendar date every year, so only the weekday changes.

Over the 8 years from 2026 to 2033, Gandhi Jayanti falls on a weekend 3 times, so on those years most people get it free of work or school without a weekday off. It lands on a Friday or Monday in 2026, 2028, which gives a long weekend. Where it falls on a Sunday, check whether your office or board gives the next day off, as practice differs.

Observing a national day

National days work best when someone has planned them. In a school, society or office, fix the programme and the chief guest three weeks before, make the list of what is needed (flags, sound, chairs, sweets) two weeks before, rehearse in the last week and check the weather and permissions two days before.

Keep the programme short. A clear start time, a few good items and a proper close are remembered longer than a long programme that drifts.

Ideas for the days before

  • Join a cleanliness drive in your area.
  • Read a few pages of "My Experiments with Truth".
  • Organise a spinning or handicraft demonstration at school.
  • Talk to children about non-violence.

Common questions

What is the date of Gandhi Jayanti next year?

Saturday, 2 October 2027.

Does the timer use my time zone?

Yes. It counts to midnight at the start of the day on your device's clock, so a visitor in another country sees a different number of hours.

Why is the date different from last year?

It is on the same calendar date every year, so only the weekday changes. The weekday in the table above shows how it shifts.
