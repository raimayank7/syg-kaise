The 45-day payment rule: sections 15, 16 and 17 of the MSMED Act

A buyer must pay a micro or small supplier on time, and the Act sets interest if not. This page follows sections 2(b), 15, 16 and 17.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 (India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 20 April 2024), and the Udyam Registration portal page on registration, as kept by the Internet Archive on 16 March 2026. We read the Act as in that copy and the portal page as archived; amendments and later portal changes were not read, so check the Udyam portal and India Code before you rely on a figure.

When the buyer must pay: section 15

Where any supplier supplies goods or renders services to a buyer, the buyer shall make payment on or before the date agreed upon between him and the supplier in writing or, where there is no agreement in this behalf, before the appointed day. The proviso says that in no case shall the period agreed upon between the supplier and the buyer in writing exceed forty-five days from the day of acceptance or the day of deemed acceptance.

The “appointed day”: section 2(b)

The appointed day means the day following immediately after the expiry of the period of fifteen days from the day of acceptance or the day of deemed acceptance of any goods or services by a buyer from a supplier.

The day of acceptance and deemed acceptance

The Explanation says the day of acceptance means (a) the day of the actual delivery of goods or rendering of services, or (b) where the buyer makes a written objection regarding acceptance within fifteen days from the day of delivery or rendering, the day on which the supplier removes that objection. The day of deemed acceptance means, where the buyer makes no written objection within fifteen days, the day of the actual delivery or rendering. So the clock runs from delivery unless the buyer objects in writing within fifteen days.

Interest if the buyer is late: section 16

Where any buyer fails to make payment as required under section 15, the buyer shall, notwithstanding anything in any agreement between buyer and supplier or in any law for the time being in force, be liable to pay compound interest with monthly rests to the supplier on that amount from the appointed day or, as the case may be, from the date immediately following the date agreed upon, at three times of the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank. We state no figure for the bank rate; it is notified by the Reserve Bank and changes. Use our calculator with the current rate.

The liability: section 17

For any goods supplied or services rendered, the buyer shall be liable to pay the amount with interest as provided under section 16.

Buyers must disclose it: section 22

Where a buyer is required to get his annual accounts audited under any law, he shall furnish additional information in his annual statement of accounts, beginning with the principal amount and the interest due on it, shown separately, remaining unpaid to any supplier at the end of each accounting year, and the amount of interest paid under section 16 along with the payments made beyond the appointed day. We read the opening of the section only.

Keep your proof

Keep the delivery challan or service completion record, the invoice, the written agreement that sets your payment period, and any written objection from the buyer with its date. Those dates decide the day of acceptance and the appointed day.

Common questions

Can we agree a 90-day payment term?

The proviso to section 15 says in no case shall the period agreed in writing exceed forty-five days from the day of acceptance or deemed acceptance.

What rate of interest applies?

Section 16 says three times the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank, as compound interest with monthly rests. The bank rate itself is not stated here.

Does this apply to every supplier?

These sections speak of a supplier and a buyer under the Act. Whether you are a supplier within the Act depends on the definitions we did not read in full, and on your Udyam registration.
