Recovering delayed payment: the Facilitation Council under section 18

If a buyer does not pay, section 18 lets the supplier go to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council. This page follows sections 18 and 19.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 (India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 20 April 2024), and the Udyam Registration portal page on registration, as kept by the Internet Archive on 16 March 2026. We read the Act as in that copy and the portal page as archived; amendments and later portal changes were not read, so check the Udyam portal and India Code before you rely on a figure.

Who can make a reference: section 18(1)

Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law, any party to a dispute may, with regard to any amount due under section 17, make a reference to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council.

Conciliation first: section 18(2)

On receipt of a reference, the Council shall either itself conduct conciliation or seek the assistance of an institution or centre providing alternate dispute resolution services by making a reference to it for conducting conciliation. Sections 65 to 81 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 apply as if the conciliation was initiated under Part III of that Act.

Then arbitration: section 18(3)

Where the conciliation is not successful and stands terminated without settlement, the Council shall either itself take up the dispute for arbitration or refer it to an institution or centre providing alternate dispute resolution services for arbitration, and the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 applies as if the arbitration was in pursuance of an arbitration agreement under section 7(1) of that Act.

Which Council: section 18(4)

The Council or the centre has jurisdiction to act as Arbitrator or Conciliator in a dispute between a supplier located within its jurisdiction and a buyer located anywhere in India. So a supplier can go to the Council where the supplier is located.

How long: section 18(5)

Every reference made under section 18 shall be decided within a period of ninety days from the date of making the reference.

Challenging the award: section 19

No application for setting aside any decree, award or other order made by the Council or the institution to which it referred the matter shall be entertained by any court unless the appellant, not being a supplier, has deposited with it seventy-five per cent of the amount in terms of the decree, award or order, in the manner directed by the court. We read the opening of the section only.

What we do not state

The form, fee and address of each State’s Council are set by State rules, which we did not read. The Ministry runs an online portal for delayed payments (MSME Samadhaan); we did not read it, so check its own instructions.

Common questions

Do I have to sue in a civil court?

Section 18(1) lets any party to a dispute make a reference to the Facilitation Council notwithstanding any other law.

How fast must the Council decide?

Section 18(5): within ninety days from the date of making the reference.
