Delayed payment interest estimate

Estimate the interest on a late payment under section 16 of the MSMED Act, 2006, using the bank rate you enter. It runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

How the estimate is worked out

  • Interest starts, if you agreed a period in writing, on the day after the agreed date (section 16); if nothing was agreed, on the appointed day, which section 2(b) defines as the day following immediately after the expiry of fifteen days from the day of acceptance. We count it as the sixteenth day after acceptance; a court or Council may count differently.
  • The yearly rate is three times the bank rate you type (section 16).
  • Compound interest with monthly rests is estimated by adding each full calendar month’s interest to the amount, and a part month as simple interest for the days over 30. Section 16 does not say how a part month is treated; this is our assumption.
  • The agreed period cannot exceed forty-five days (proviso to section 15), so the tool accepts at most 45.

Read the rules on our page on sections 15 and 16.

Common questions

Which bank rate do I type?

Section 16 says three times the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank. We do not state that rate, because it changes. Find the current notified bank rate on the Reserve Bank of India site and type it here; the tool multiplies it by three.

Is the result what a Council will award?

No. It is an estimate under our reading of sections 2(b) and 16, with the assumptions listed on the page. The Facilitation Council or the court decides the amount.
