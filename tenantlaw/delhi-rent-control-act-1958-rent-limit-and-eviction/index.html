Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958: who it covers and the grounds of eviction

Delhi’s older rent law still applies to cheaper premises. This page follows sections 1, 3, 4 and 14 of the Act.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Source read on 14 November 2026: the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 (India Code copy, 31 pages, through an Internet Archive copy of 3 April 2026). We read the Act text named on the page, not the rules made under it, the schedule of areas, later amendments or any court judgment. State rent laws are amended; check the current text and your local rent authority or court before you act.

Where it applies: section 1(2)

The Act extends to the areas within the limits of the New Delhi Municipal Committee and the Delhi Cantonment Board and to such urban areas within the limits of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi as are specified in the First Schedule, which we did not read. The Central Government may extend or exclude areas by notification.

Premises it does not cover: section 3

Nothing in the Act applies to (a) premises belonging to the Government; (b) tenancies created by a grant from the Government for premises taken on lease or requisitioned by it (with a proviso for premises lawfully let by others); (c) any premises, residential or not, whose monthly rent exceeds three thousand and five hundred rupees; or (d) premises constructed on or after the commencement of the Delhi Rent Control (Amendment) Act, 1988, for ten years from the date of completion. So most modern lettings fall outside this Act.

Rent above standard rent: section 4(1)

Except where rent is liable to periodical increase under an agreement made before 1 January 1939, no tenant shall, notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary, be liable to pay his landlord for occupation of any premises any amount in excess of the standard rent, unless it is a lawful increase of the standard rent in accordance with the Act. We did not read how standard rent is fixed.

Eviction only on listed grounds: section 14(1)

No order or decree for recovery of possession shall be made by any court or Controller in favour of the landlord against a tenant, but the Controller may on application order recovery on one or more of the grounds listed in the proviso. Those we read include: (a) the tenant has neither paid nor tendered the whole of the arrears of rent legally recoverable within two months of the date on which a notice of demand was served in the manner of section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882; (b) the tenant has, on or after 9 June 1952, sub-let, assigned or parted with possession without the landlord’s written consent; (c) the tenant has used the premises for a purpose other than that for which they were let, without the landlord’s written consent; (d) premises let as a residence were not lived in by the tenant or his family for six months immediately before the application; (e) premises let for residence are required bona fide by the landlord for himself or a dependent family member, who has no other reasonably suitable residential accommodation; (f) the premises have become unsafe or unfit for habitation and are required bona fide for repairs that need vacation; (g) the premises are required bona fide for building or rebuilding; (h) the tenant has acquired vacant possession of or been allotted another residence; (i) the premises were let as a residence by reason of employment and the employment ended; (j) the tenant has caused or permitted substantial damage. The proviso has further grounds that we did not read.

Common questions

Does the Delhi Rent Control Act apply to my flat?

Not if the monthly rent exceeds Rs 3,500 (section 3(c)), or the premises were built on or after the 1988 amendment’s commencement and are within ten years of completion (section 3(d)). It also covers only the areas in section 1(2) and its First Schedule.

How long can a tenant be in arrears before eviction?

Section 14(1)(a): ground exists if the tenant has neither paid nor tendered the whole arrears within two months of service of the notice of demand.
