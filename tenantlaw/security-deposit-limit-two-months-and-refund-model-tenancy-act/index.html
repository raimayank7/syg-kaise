Security deposit under the Model Tenancy Act: clauses 11, 12 and 14

The Model Act caps the security deposit and says when it is returned. This page follows clauses 11, 12 and 14.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 14 November 2026: the Model Tenancy Act, 2021 (Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, 28 pages, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 15 May 2026) and the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, 2021 (India Code copy, 28 pages, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 8 November 2025). The Model Tenancy Act is a model: it binds nobody unless a State enacts it or adopts its provisions, and tenancy is a State matter. We read only the clauses named on each page, and, beyond the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, we read only the State laws of Tamil Nadu, Delhi, Maharashtra and Karnataka (see their pages), so the rule that applies to your flat is the law of your State. Ask a lawyer or the local rent authority.

The cap: clause 11(1)

The security deposit to be paid by the tenant in advance shall be such as may be agreed between the landlord and the tenant in the tenancy agreement, which shall (a) not exceed two months’ rent in the case of residential premises, and (b) not exceed six months’ rent in the case of non-residential premises. The Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, 2021, section 11, carries the same words.

The refund: clause 11(2)

The security deposit shall be refunded to the tenant on the date of taking over vacant possession of the premises from the tenant, after making due deduction of any liability of the tenant.

Two signed copies: clause 12

The tenancy agreement shall be signed in duplicate by both the landlord and the tenant, and one original signed copy shall be retained by each.

If the landlord will not take the rent: clause 14(1) and (2)

Where the landlord refuses to accept the rent and other charges, or refuses to give a receipt, the rent shall be paid to the landlord by postal money order or any other method as prescribed, for two consecutive months; if the landlord still refuses, the tenant may deposit it with the Rent Authority as prescribed. Where the tenant is unable to decide to whom the rent is payable during the tenancy, the tenant may deposit the rent with the Rent Authority as prescribed. We did not read the rest of clause 14 or the prescribed manner.

Practical points

  • Put the deposit amount in the agreement and ask for a receipt.
  • Note the date the vacant possession is handed over; clause 11(2) ties the refund to that date.
  • Keep proof of every rent payment; if the landlord refuses to accept rent, write down the dates and the method you used.
  • The Model Tenancy Act is a model law. We read it as the Ministry published it; a State’s own Act may differ.

Common questions

How much deposit can a landlord ask?

Under clause 11(1) of the Model Act, not more than two months’ rent for residential premises and six months for non-residential premises. Whether your State’s law says the same depends on the State.

When must the deposit come back?

Clause 11(2): on the date of taking over vacant possession, after due deduction of any liability of the tenant.
