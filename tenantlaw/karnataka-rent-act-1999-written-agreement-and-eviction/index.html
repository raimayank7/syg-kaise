Karnataka Rent Act, 1999: written agreement, rent payment and eviction

Karnataka has had its own Rent Act in force since 31 December 2001 for the areas in its First Schedule. This page follows sections 1, 2, 4, 5, 16, 27 and 28.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Source read on 14 November 2026: the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 (Karnataka Act 34 of 2001), the copy on the Karnataka legislative site dpal.kar.nic.in as kept by the Internet Archive on 29 March 2017. It notes it was amended by Act 17 of 2007; later amendments were not read. We read the Act text named on the page, not the rules made under it, the schedule of areas, later amendments or any court judgment. State rent laws are amended; check the current text and your local rent authority or court before you act.

In force and where: sections 1 and 2

The copy notes that the Act came into force on 31 December 2001 by Notification No. RD 24 BHANIVI 2001 dated 5 December 2001. Section 2 says Chapters I to III and V to VIII apply to the areas in the First Schedule, which we did not read. Section 2(3) says nothing in the Act applies to premises belonging to the State or Central Government or a local authority, to a Muzarai, religious or charitable institution or a Wakf, to buildings of registered cooperative societies and of Market Committees, and to other premises we did not read in full.

Only a written tenancy: section 4

Section 4(1): notwithstanding section 107 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, no person shall, after commencement, let or take on rent any premises except by an agreement in writing. For earlier tenancies without a written agreement, the landlord and tenant shall enter into one and deposit a copy before the prescribed authority; if they fail to present it jointly, each shall separately file the particulars in the prescribed form.

When the tenant dies: section 5

The right of tenancy devolves for ten years from the tenant’s death to his successors in this order: spouse; son or daughter; parents; daughter-in-law who is the widow of a pre-deceased son, provided the successor had ordinarily lived or carried on business in the premises with the tenant and was dependent on him (with further provisos we did not read in full).

Paying rent and receipts: section 16

The tenant shall pay rent and other charges within the time fixed by contract or, absent that, by the fifteenth day of the month next following the month for which it is payable; on default the tenant is liable to simple interest at twelve per cent per annum from the date it became payable. A tenant who pays rent or advance shall be entitled to a signed written receipt, though the tenant may remit by postal money order.

Eviction on listed grounds only: section 27

Section 27(1): no order or decree for recovery of possession shall be made in favour of the landlord against a tenant save as in section 27(2). The Court may order recovery on one or more of these grounds only, which include: (a) the tenant has neither paid nor tendered the whole arrears of rent and charges within two months of the service of a notice of demand under section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act (with provisos: the notice benefit is lost on a second default, and a tenant to be evicted is ordered to vacate unless he pays or deposits the arrears within one month of the order); (b) sub-letting or parting with possession without written consent; (c) use for a purpose other than that let, after a registered-post notice and a month to stop, where the misuse is a public nuisance or damages the landlord’s interest; and (d) a ground about occupation of the premises by the tenant or his family (we did not read it in full). We read the opening grounds only.

Refund of advance rent: section 28(2)

Where the landlord recovers possession under the section 28 right or under sections 27, 29, 30 or 31, and had received rent in advance, he shall within ninety days from recovery of possession refund the rent for the unexpired portion of the lease; for any other payment, refund the proportion that the unexpired portion bears to the whole period. The proviso makes a landlord who defaults liable to simple interest at twelve per cent per annum on the amount not refunded.

Common questions

Is an oral tenancy allowed in Karnataka?

Section 4(1): no person shall let or take on rent any premises except by an agreement in writing after commencement of the Act.

What interest applies if rent is late?

Section 16(1): simple interest at twelve per cent per annum from the date it becomes payable, which is by the fifteenth of the next month unless the contract fixes a time.
