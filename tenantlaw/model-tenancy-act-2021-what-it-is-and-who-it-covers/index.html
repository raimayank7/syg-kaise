Model Tenancy Act 2021: what it is and what it covers

The Model Tenancy Act, 2021 is a template that States can adopt. This page explains what it is, what it leaves out and its first rule: a written agreement, told to the Rent Authority.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 14 November 2026: the Model Tenancy Act, 2021 (Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, 28 pages, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 15 May 2026) and the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, 2021 (India Code copy, 28 pages, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 8 November 2025). The Model Tenancy Act is a model: it binds nobody unless a State enacts it or adopts its provisions, and tenancy is a State matter. We read only the clauses named on each page, and, beyond the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, we read only the State laws of Tamil Nadu, Delhi, Maharashtra and Karnataka (see their pages), so the rule that applies to your flat is the law of your State. Ask a lawyer or the local rent authority.

A model, not a national law

Its rule-making clause speaks of the “State Government/Union territory Administration” making rules, which fits a text meant to be enacted by each State or Union territory. We have not read the cabinet decision, so we say only that it is a model text circulated for adoption; tenancy remains a State matter. The Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, 2021 (India Code copy) carries the same structure and, in the sections we compared, the same words, and its clause 1(3) says it comes into force on a date the State Government appoints by notification; we did not find that notification.

Premises it does not apply to: clause 3(1)

Nothing in the Model Act applies to (a) premises owned or promoted by the Central Government, a State Government, a local authority, a Government undertaking or enterprise, a statutory body or a Cantonment Board; (b) premises owned by a company, University or organization given on rent to its employees as part of a service contract; (c) premises owned by religious or charitable institutions as may be specified by the State Government by notification; (d) premises owned by auqaf registered under the Waqf Act, 1995 or by a trust registered under the State’s public trust law; and (e) other buildings or categories the clause goes on to list, which we read only in outline.

Only by a written agreement: clause 4(1)

No person shall, after the commencement of the Act, let or take on rent any premises except by an agreement in writing, which shall be informed to the Rent Authority by the landlord and tenant jointly, in the form specified in the First Schedule, within two months from the date of the tenancy agreement.

If they do not inform together: clause 4(2)

If they fail to inform jointly, the landlord and the tenant shall separately inform the execution of the agreement to the Rent Authority within one month from the date of expiry of the two-month period.

What the Rent Authority does: clause 4(3), (4)

The Rent Authority shall put in place a digital platform in the local vernacular language within three months of its appointment. After receiving the information and the documents in the First Schedule it shall give a unique identification number to the parties and upload details of the agreement on its website within seven working days.

Why informing matters: clause 4(6)

The information given under clause 4(1) and (2) shall be conclusive proof of the facts relating to the tenancy, and in the absence of any statement of information the landlord and tenant are not entitled to any relief under the Act.

Period of tenancy: clause 5(1), (2)

Every tenancy entered into after commencement shall be valid for the period agreed between the landlord and the tenant and specified in the tenancy agreement. The tenant may request renewal or extension within the agreed period and, if the landlord agrees, they may enter a new agreement on mutually agreed terms.

Common questions

Does the Model Tenancy Act apply in my State?

Only if your State has enacted it or adopted its provisions. We read the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, 2021 and found its commencement left to a notification; we did not read other States.

Is an oral tenancy valid under it?

Clause 4(1) says no person shall let or take on rent any premises except by an agreement in writing, and clause 4(6) says without the information, no relief under the Act.
