Landlord entry and eviction: clauses 17, 21 and 22 of the Model Act

Under the Model Act a landlord cannot simply walk in or throw a tenant out. This page follows clauses 17, 21 and 22.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 14 November 2026: the Model Tenancy Act, 2021 (Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, 28 pages, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 15 May 2026) and the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, 2021 (India Code copy, 28 pages, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 8 November 2025). The Model Tenancy Act is a model: it binds nobody unless a State enacts it or adopts its provisions, and tenancy is a State matter. We read only the clauses named on each page, and, beyond the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, we read only the State laws of Tamil Nadu, Delhi, Maharashtra and Karnataka (see their pages), so the rule that applies to your flat is the law of your State. Ask a lawyer or the local rent authority.

Entry with notice: clause 17(1), (2)

Every landlord or property manager may enter the premises after serving a notice in writing or through electronic mode on the tenant at least twenty-four hours before the time of entry, (a) to carry out repairs or replacement, (b) to inspect whether the premises are habitable, or (c) for any other reasonable cause specified in the tenancy agreement. The notice shall specify the day, time and reason for entry. We read the opening of the proviso to sub-section (2) only.

No eviction during the tenancy except by the Court: clause 21(1)

A tenant shall not be evicted during the continuance of the tenancy agreement unless otherwise agreed in writing by the landlord and tenant, except in accordance with clause 21(2) or clause 22.

Grounds the Rent Court may order eviction on: clause 21(2)

The Rent Court, on an application by the landlord, may order eviction and recovery of possession on one or more of these grounds: (a) the tenant does not agree to pay the rent payable under clause 8; (b) the tenant has not paid the arrears of rent and other charges in full for two consecutive months, including interest for delayed payment as specified in the agreement, within one month from the date of service of a notice of demand in the manner provided in section 106(4) of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882; (c) the tenant has, after commencement, parted with possession of the whole or any part of the premises without the landlord’s written consent; (d) the tenant has continued to misuse the premises even after receipt of notice from the landlord to desist, where “misuse” means encroachment of additional space, use that causes public nuisance or damage or is detrimental to the landlord’s interest, or for immoral or illegal purposes; and (e) a ground where the landlord needs to carry out repair, construction, rebuilding or demolition (we read the opening of that ground only), with further grounds that we did not read in full.

When the landlord dies: clause 22

Where the landlord dies and the legal heirs have a bona fide requirement of the premises during the tenancy, the legal heirs may apply to the Rent Court for eviction and recovery of possession; if the Court is satisfied that the requirement is bona fide, it may order the tenant to hand over vacant possession to the legal heirs.

Where this stops

These are the Model Act’s clauses. Eviction in your State follows your State’s law and courts. We did not read any State’s eviction rules other than the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act’s overall structure, and we did not read what happens after an order. Ask a lawyer before you act on a notice.

The Model Tenancy Act is a model law. We read it as the Ministry published it; a State’s own Act may differ.

Common questions

Can a landlord enter the flat any time?

Under clause 17 of the Model Act, only after a written or electronic notice at least twenty-four hours before, naming the day, time and reason.

Can a landlord evict me for one month’s late rent?

Clause 21(2)(b) speaks of arrears of rent for two consecutive months, a notice of demand, and a further period of one month from its service, with the Rent Court deciding. A State law may differ.
