Letter to the landlord asking for the security deposit back (sample)

A written request, with the dates of handing over, so there is a record before any other step.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

To, [Name of the landlord] [Address] Date: [Date] Subject: Refund of security deposit for [Address of the rented premises] Dear Sir/Madam, 1. I took the premises at [Address of the rented premises] on rent from you under the tenancy agreement dated [Date of agreement]. I paid a security deposit of Rs [Amount] on [Date of payment] by [Cash / Bank transfer / UPI / Cheque]. 2. I handed over vacant possession of the premises to you on [Date of handing over]. [Describe the condition: for example, the premises were inspected on that day and keys were handed over to [Name of person].] 3. The deposit is due to be refunded on the date of taking over vacant possession, after deduction of any liability I owe, if any. [State any amount you accept as due, or: I do not owe any rent or charges.] Please refund Rs [Amount claimed] to [Your bank account or other payment details] and send me any statement of deductions in writing. 4. If I do not receive it within [Number] days, I will take the steps open to me under the law. Yours sincerely, ______________________ [Your full name] [Your address and phone] Enclosures: [List the agreement and payment proof]

What this sample is for

A written request, with the dates of handing over, so there is a record before any other step.

Which parts of the Act it follows

  • Clause 11(1) of the Model Tenancy Act: deposit agreed in the agreement, not above two months’ rent (residential) or six months’ (non-residential).
  • Clause 11(2): the deposit shall be refunded on the date of taking over vacant possession, after due deduction of any liability of the tenant.
  • The Model Act is a model; your State’s law may differ and may set a different cap or process.

Check before you send

  • Attach the agreement and the proof of deposit.
  • State the date you handed over possession, and keep any receipt for the keys.
  • Do not threaten; state the date and the amount.
  • Check your State’s rent law or ask a lawyer before you go to court or the rent authority.

Sources

Read on 14 November 2026: the Model Tenancy Act, 2021 (Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, 28 pages, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 15 May 2026) and the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, 2021 (India Code copy, 28 pages, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 8 November 2025). The Model Tenancy Act is a model: it binds nobody unless a State enacts it or adopts its provisions, and tenancy is a State matter. We read only the clauses named on each page, and, beyond the Uttarakhand Tenancy Act, we read only the State laws of Tamil Nadu, Delhi, Maharashtra and Karnataka (see their pages), so the rule that applies to your flat is the law of your State. Ask a lawyer or the local rent authority.
