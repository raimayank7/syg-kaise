What a notary can do: section 8

Section 8(1) of the Notaries Act, 1952 lists what a notary may do. This page puts the list into plain English.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Notaries Act, 1952 (India Code copy).

The list in section 8(1)

The section says a notary may do all or any of these acts by virtue of office:

  • (a) verify, authenticate, certify or attest the execution of any instrument;
  • (b) present any promissory note, hundi or bill of exchange for acceptance or payment or demand better security;
  • (c) note or protest the dishonour of such instruments or serve notice of the protest;
  • (d) note and draw up ship's protest, boat's protest or protests relating to demurrage and other commercial matters;
  • (e) administer oath to, or take affidavit from, any person;
  • (f) prepare bottomry and respondentia bonds, charter parties and other mercantile documents;
  • (g) prepare, attest or authenticate any instrument intended to take effect outside India, in the form and language that conform to the law of the place;
  • (h) translate, and verify the translation of, any document from one language into another;
  • (ha) act as a Commissioner to record evidence in any civil or criminal trial if directed by a court or authority; (hb) act as an arbitrator, mediator or conciliator, if required;
  • (i) any other act which may be prescribed.

The seal and signature rule

Section 8(2) says no act in section 8(1) is deemed a notarial act "except when it is done by a notary under his signature and official seal". An attestation without the notary's signature and seal is not a notarial act.

What it means for you

Two items matter most for individuals: (a), attesting the execution of an instrument such as an agreement, and (e), taking an affidavit. Item (h) lets a notary verify a translation. A notary attests that you signed or swore; the notary does not check that the contents of your document are lawful or complete.

Appointment and rules

The Act and the rules made under it also deal with appointment and conduct of notaries and with fees. We read the section on functions only, so we state nothing about fees; ask the notary and your state.

Common questions

Does a notary check that my statements are true?

Section 8(1)(e) says a notary administers the oath or takes the affidavit. The truth of what you swear remains your responsibility.
