How to get an affidavit attested

A short checklist for getting an affidavit signed, sworn and attested, built around what the Acts say.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Oaths Act, 1969 (India Code copy, "As on the 6th Oct, 2025"); the Notaries Act, 1952 (India Code copy); the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (India Code copy).

Step 1: ask the office what it wants

The office that asked for the affidavit decides what the statement must say and who must attest it. Ask for its format if it has one. The Acts do not prescribe the contents of an affidavit for ordinary uses; they set the form of the verification (Form No. 4 of the Schedule to the Oaths Act) and say who may administer the oath.

Step 2: write only facts you know

  • Use your full name, parentage, age and address as in your identity documents.
  • Number the paragraphs; each should state one fact.
  • Do not copy long documents into it; refer to them as annexures.
  • Leave no blanks and no corrections. Rewrite instead of crossing out.

Step 3: stamp paper

Section 35 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 says an instrument chargeable with duty cannot be admitted in evidence, acted upon, registered or authenticated by a public officer unless it is duly stamped. Whether an affidavit is chargeable, and with what, depends on the Schedule as it applies in your state, which this site does not state. Ask the notary or office, and buy stamp paper only from authorised vendors.

Step 4: sign before the notary

Do not sign in advance. Take the unsigned document and your identity proof, read it to the notary or let the notary read it, then swear or affirm and sign. Section 8(2) of the Notaries Act says a notarial act is done under the notary's signature and official seal, so look for both on your copy.

Step 5: keep proof

Ask for the entry number or receipt the notary gives, and keep a scan. If the office needs the original, make copies first.

Common questions

Can someone else sign my affidavit for me?

Form No. 4 says "this is my name and signature (or mark)": the deponent swears to their own signature or mark. Ask the notary what to do if you cannot sign.
