Oath or solemn affirmation

The Oaths Act lets you either swear in the name of God or solemnly affirm. Here is what the sections say.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Oaths Act, 1969 (India Code copy, "As on the 6th Oct, 2025").

Affirmation is a free choice

Section 5 says that a witness, interpreter or juror "may, instead of making an oath, make an affirmation". The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Act explains that this was meant to give every such person the liberty to affirm, whatever their religion and whether or not they object to an oath. In the Schedule, the printed form reads "I do swear in the name of God" or "solemnly affirm", so an affidavit is made in one of these two ways.

Who must swear or affirm

Section 4(1) says oaths or affirmations shall be made by witnesses, interpreters and jurors. The proviso to section 4(1) deals with a child witness under twelve years: if the court thinks the child understands the duty of speaking the truth but not the nature of an oath, the oath provisions do not apply and the absence of an oath does not make the evidence inadmissible. Section 4(2) says an accused person is not sworn in a criminal proceeding unless examined as a witness for the defence.

Forms

Section 6(1) says oaths and affirmations made under section 4 are administered according to the form in the Schedule that suits the case. The proviso lets a witness in a judicial proceeding take any other form held binding by persons of the class to which he belongs, if it is not repugnant to justice or decency and does not purport to affect a third person, and the court allows it.

If something goes wrong in the form

Section 7 says that no omission to take an oath or affirmation, no substitution of one for the other, and no irregularity in administering it or in its form shall invalidate any proceeding or make evidence inadmissible, or affect the witness's obligation to state the truth. Section 8 says every person giving evidence before a court or authorised person is bound to state the truth. Neither section is a licence to be careless: always read your affidavit before swearing it.

Common questions

Do I have to say "God" in my affidavit?

The Schedule form allows "solemnly affirm" in place of "swear in the name of God". Choose the form that matches your conscience and cross out the other if the form prints both.
