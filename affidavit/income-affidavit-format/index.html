Income affidavit format

A sworn statement of your yearly income from named sources, for an office or school that asks for an income affidavit instead of a certificate.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

AFFIDAVIT (Income) I, [Full name], son/daughter/wife of [Father's or husband's name], aged [Age] years, resident of [Full address], do hereby solemnly affirm and state on oath as follows: 1. That I am [Occupation]. 2. That my annual income from all sources for the year [Year] was Rs [Amount] from [Source 1] and Rs [Amount] from [Source 2]. 3. That I have no other source of income. 4. That I am making this affidavit for submission to [Office] for [Purpose]. DEPONENT VERIFICATION I, the above-named deponent, do swear in the name of God / solemnly affirm that this is my name and signature (or mark) and that the contents of this my affidavit are true. Nothing material has been concealed. Verified at [Place] on [Date]. DEPONENT [To be attested by the notary or other person empowered to administer the oath, under signature and official seal.]

What this sample is for

A sworn statement of your yearly income from named sources, for an office or school that asks for an income affidavit instead of a certificate.

Each numbered paragraph below should be one fact that you know yourself. For court cases, Order XIX Rule 3(1) of the Code of Civil Procedure confines affidavits to facts the deponent can prove of his own knowledge, with statements of belief admitted on interlocutory applications if the grounds are stated.

Check before you sign

  • Write the figures you can support with a payslip, return or bank record.
  • Add the amounts and check that the total matches what you will state elsewhere.
  • The office may want a certificate from a particular authority instead. Ask.

Who can attest it

Section 3(2) of the Oaths Act, 1969 lets persons empowered by the High Court or the State Government administer oaths and affirmations for affidavits, and section 8(1)(e) of the Notaries Act, 1952 lets a notary administer an oath or take an affidavit; under section 8(2) the act is notarial only when done under the notary's signature and official seal. Form No. 4 in the Schedule to the Oaths Act gives the verification: "I do swear in the name of God / solemnly affirm that this is my name and signature (or mark) and that the contents of this my affidavit are true."

Stamp paper and fees

This site states no stamp-paper value, notary fee or state rule, because they differ by state and change. Section 35 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 says an instrument chargeable with duty cannot be admitted in evidence, acted upon, registered or authenticated by a public officer unless duly stamped. Ask the notary or the office whether and how your affidavit is chargeable.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Oaths Act, 1969 (as on 6 October 2025); the Notaries Act, 1952; the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Order XIX; the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, section 35.

Common questions

Is this format accepted everywhere?

No. It is a general sample. The office or court decides what it needs, and may have its own form.

Sample, not legal advice. Use only facts that are true. The office that asked for the affidavit decides its wording.
