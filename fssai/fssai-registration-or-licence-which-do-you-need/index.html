FSSAI registration or licence: which one do you need?

Anyone who makes, stores, sells or serves food has to be registered or licensed. This page explains the three levels and how to tell them apart, from FSSAI’s own FAQs.

Sources

Sources read on 14 November 2026: the FSSAI FoSCoS “FAQs on FSSAI License and Registration” (page footer: last updated 21 June 2021), and the FoSCoS “Kind of Business” eligibility and fee table (a PDF whose own properties give the date 16 November 2023). Both documents are older than today; FSSAI can change fees, limits and rules, so check the FoSCoS portal (foscos.fssai.gov.in) or call its help desk before you apply.

Three levels

The FAQs say that under the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations, 2011, licences and registrations are granted to food business operators in a three-tier system: Registration, State licence and Central licence. The eligibility criteria are on the home page of the Food Safety Compliance System (FoSCoS).

Who needs only a registration

The FAQs define a petty food business operator as one who himself manufactures or sells any article of food, or a petty retailer, hawker, itinerant vendor or temporary stall holder, or a small scale or cottage or other such industry relating to food, or a tiny food business operator, having an annual turnover up to Rs 12 lakh and/or whose production capacity of food does not exceed 100 kg or litres a day, or whose handling and collection of milk is up to 500 litres a day, or whose slaughtering capacity is 2 large animals, 10 small animals or 50 poultry birds a day or less. These operators obtain a registration certificate on the Food Licensing and Registration System.

How to tell the number apart

A licence number has 14 digits and starts with 1; a registration number also has 14 digits and starts with 2. The first three digits of a Central licence number are 100. The certificate carries a QR code that shows the details held in FSSAI’s database, which helps to spot fraud.

One premises, one licence

The FAQs say a licence is based on the premises: all kinds of food business at one premises can go on one licence, and no operator is allowed more than one licence for a single premises. If you hold one State licence and one Central licence for the same premises, you surrender the State licence and apply to modify the Central licence to add the kind of business.

Pharmacies, chemists and grocers

The FAQs say pharmacies and medical stores selling health supplements or nutraceutical products need a licence or registration, and that chemists, general or grocery retailers need one if they sell food products. Wine and liquor retail shops are included, because alcohol falls under the definition of food in section 3(n) of the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006.

Not covered by a licence

Utensils and packaging material need no FSSAI licence, because they are not “food” under section 3(n) of the Act, according to the FAQs.

Next step

Find your kind of business and the fee on our table page, then apply on FoSCoS. We do not apply for you, and FSSAI’s own FAQs say there is no need to submit physical documents at the regional or State office after the online application.

Common questions

How do I know if my number is a licence or a registration?

Both have 14 digits. A licence starts with 1 and a registration starts with 2, according to FSSAI’s FAQs.

Can I hold a State licence and a Central licence for the same shop?

The FAQs say no: a licence is for one premises and you may not hold two. Surrender the State licence and modify the Central one.

Does a tea stall need a licence?

A stall with annual turnover up to Rs 12 lakh falls among the petty food business operators and needs a registration. Above that, the table page shows the licence.
