How to print at actual size

Printable paper is only useful if the squares and lines come out the size you chose. The printer settings decide that.

The one setting that matters

Printers have a "Fit" or "Shrink to fit" option that scales the page to the paper. Our pages are drawn at the exact paper size, so you want 100% scale, "Actual size", or "Default" scaling.

Chrome and Edge

  • Press Ctrl+P.
  • Under More settings, set Scale to Default (100%) and Margins to None.
  • Make sure the paper size matches your sheet (A4 or Letter).
  • Print.

Firefox

  • Press Ctrl+P and choose More settings.
  • Set Scale to 100% and untick Shrink to fit.
  • Set margins to None.

Windows PDF and printer dialogs

  • In the printer's own Properties, find "Scaling" or "Fit to page" and turn it off.
  • Pick the right paper tray and type.
  • Do not choose "Borderless" unless you want the edges cropped.

Check with a ruler

After the first print, lay a ruler across the grid. Ten squares of a 5 mm grid must measure 50 mm. If it is off by more than a millimetre, the scaling is not at 100%.

Saving as a PDF

In the print dialog, choose "Save as PDF" as the printer. The PDF keeps the true size and can be printed later from any device.

When the numbers still look wrong

If the squares are the right size but the page looks cropped, your printer may add its own unprintable margin of a few millimetres. Increase the margin in the form to 10 or 12 mm, which the preview shows as a larger blank border, so no lines fall in the area the printer cannot reach. If the page prints in the wrong orientation, check that the orientation in the print dialog matches the one chosen in the form: the page size rule on this site sets the paper shape, but a printer can still rotate a page on its own. If you print many copies, keep the dialog settings saved as a preset in your browser or printer driver.

Common questions

Why are my squares smaller than expected?

Shrink to fit was on, or the paper size in the dialog did not match the page.
