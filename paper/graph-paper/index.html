What graph paper is for

Graph paper is paper printed with a regular grid of squares. It keeps lines straight and sizes in proportion, so it is used for plotting graphs in maths and science, for scale drawings in design and engineering, for pixel art and cross-stitch patterns, and for neat handwriting and tables.

Most of the grid is drawn in a light line. Every fifth line (you can change this) is drawn heavier, so you can count in fives and find your place quickly.

How to choose the grid

  • 5 mm squares suit most schoolwork and general drawing.
  • 10 mm (1 cm) squares suit young children and large plots.
  • 2 mm or 1 mm suit engineering sketches and precise scientific plots; use a thin, light line so it does not swamp your pencil.
  • 6.35 mm (quarter inch) is the common US grid; the page for it has the preset.

Printing tips

Print at "Actual size" or 100%, not "Fit to page", or the squares will not be the size you chose. Our guide on printing at actual size explains the setting in Chrome, Edge and Windows. Check one square with a ruler after the first print.

Paper and pen

Use a hard pencil (HB or H) for plotting so lines stay thin and erase cleanly. Mark axes with a ruler and label the unit on each. If the graph will be photocopied, choose black or dark grey for the grid, because pale blue and green can vanish on a copy. Keep a few spare sheets: a first plot often shows the scale was wrong.

How much fits with these settings

Using the settings this page starts with (margin 10 mm), a sheet holds: A4 portrait: 38 across and 55 down (2090 squares); A5 portrait: 25 across and 38 down (950 squares); A3 portrait: 55 across and 80 down (4400 squares); Letter portrait: 39 across and 51 down (1989 squares). Change the numbers in the form and the preview updates; the line under the preview states the count for your own settings. Landscape swaps the two numbers.

Common questions

Can I print on A4 and get Letter?

Pick the page size in the form to match your paper. The squares are drawn in millimetres, so they come out the same size on either.

Are the squares exactly square?

Yes. The page is drawn in true millimetres. Printers add a small unprinted edge, so keep a margin of at least 5 mm.
