Millimetre graph paper

Millimetre paper has a grid of 1 mm squares. This version sets a very thin line so the grid does not drown your pencil.

What it is used for

Millimetre paper is used for precise plotting in physics and chemistry practicals, calibration curves, engineering drawings and sketches where you must read values to the nearest millimetre. Some labs also use it for tracing outlines of leaves or objects at true size.

Settings that matter

At this density a thick line makes the page grey. The preset uses a 0.08 mm line and a bold line every 10 mm. If your printer cannot render so thin a line, raise the weight to 0.12 and use a lighter colour. Test one print before printing a pack.

Printing

Printing at actual size is vital: an enlarged page makes the millimetres wrong. Check with a ruler against the printed grid; 10 squares must measure 10 mm.

Paper and pen

Use a hard pencil (HB or H) for plotting so lines stay thin and erase cleanly. Mark axes with a ruler and label the unit on each. If the graph will be photocopied, choose black or dark grey for the grid, because pale blue and green can vanish on a copy. Keep a few spare sheets: a first plot often shows the scale was wrong.

How much fits with these settings

Using the settings this page starts with (margin 10 mm), a sheet holds: A4 portrait: 190 across and 277 down (52630 squares); A5 portrait: 128 across and 190 down (24320 squares); A3 portrait: 277 across and 400 down (110800 squares); Letter portrait: 195 across and 259 down (50505 squares). Change the numbers in the form and the preview updates; the line under the preview states the count for your own settings. Landscape swaps the two numbers.

Common questions

Why does my print look grey?

The grid is very fine. Lower the weight or choose a lighter colour.
