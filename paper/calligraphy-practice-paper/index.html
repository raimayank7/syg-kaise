Printable Paper

Printable calligraphy practice paper

Calligraphy uses lines measured in nib widths. Set your nib width and slant and print guide sheets.

The guide lines

In broad-edge calligraphy the height of letters is counted in nib widths. This sheet draws eight bands of the width you enter, with heavier lines at the waistline and baseline, and slanted lines at the angle you set (55 degrees is a common slant for italic hands; many foundational hands use 5 degrees or none). Measure your nib by drawing a row of side-by-side squares and adjust the number.

Using the sheet

  • Put the sheet under a lighter plain sheet if you want to see lines through it.
  • Practice basic strokes first: vertical lines, curves, diagonal lines.
  • Keep the pen angle steady; the guide lines help you see if it drifts.
  • Move on to letters when strokes are even.

Printing

Print on smooth paper that does not feather: laser printers on 90 gsm or heavier are good. A light-grey line is easy to ignore while you write.

Paper and pen

Check the nib width by a row of squares drawn with the pen held at its writing angle, then set the same width here. Practise strokes on one sheet and letters on another. Use a smooth, ink-friendly paper; cheap copy paper can feather with wet ink.

How much fits with these settings

Using the settings this page starts with (margin 12 mm), a sheet holds: A4 portrait: printable area 186 by 273 mm; A5 portrait: printable area 124 by 186 mm; A3 portrait: printable area 273 by 396 mm; Letter portrait: printable area 191 by 255 mm. Change the numbers in the form and the preview updates; the line under the preview states the count for your own settings. Landscape swaps the two numbers.

Common questions

Can I use it for brush pens?

Yes. Use the lines as guides for height and slant, and ignore the nib-width idea.
