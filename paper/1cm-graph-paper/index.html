1 cm graph paper

Squares of 1 cm are easy to count and easy to see. This page opens the graph paper maker with a 10 mm grid.

When 1 cm helps

Large squares suit young children learning to plot points, posters and display charts, and any graph that must be read from a distance. They are also good for coordinate geometry in the first years of school, where each square stands for one unit.

Making a poster

Choose A3 and print once, or print four A4 sheets and tape them. Leave the margin at 10 mm so the sheets join cleanly. For a number line or axes, darken the line along the middle with a pen.

Classroom use

  • Plot points and join them to make shapes.
  • Teach area by counting squares.
  • Draw bar charts of class data.
  • Practise symmetry by folding the paper.

Paper and pen

Use a hard pencil (HB or H) for plotting so lines stay thin and erase cleanly. Mark axes with a ruler and label the unit on each. If the graph will be photocopied, choose black or dark grey for the grid, because pale blue and green can vanish on a copy. Keep a few spare sheets: a first plot often shows the scale was wrong.

How much fits with these settings

Using the settings this page starts with (margin 10 mm), a sheet holds: A4 portrait: 19 across and 27 down (513 squares); A5 portrait: 12 across and 19 down (228 squares); A3 portrait: 27 across and 40 down (1080 squares); Letter portrait: 19 across and 25 down (475 squares). Change the numbers in the form and the preview updates; the line under the preview states the count for your own settings. Landscape swaps the two numbers.

Common questions

How many squares fit on A4?

With a 10 mm margin, 19 across and 27 down.
