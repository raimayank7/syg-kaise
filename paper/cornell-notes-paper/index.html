Printable Cornell notes paper

The Cornell method splits a page into cues, notes and a summary. Print the layout and use it in lectures and revision.

The method

The Cornell note-taking system was devised at Cornell University for students. The page has a wide notes area on the right, a narrow cue column on the left and a summary strip along the bottom. During the lecture you write notes on the right. Afterwards you write keywords or questions in the cue column, and a two or three line summary at the bottom.

How to use the page

  • Write the topic and date in the top strip.
  • Take notes in your own words on the right.
  • Within a day, write cue words or questions on the left.
  • Cover the notes and use the cues to test yourself.
  • Write a summary of the page in your own words.

Measurements

The cue column is 63.5 mm (2.5 inches) wide and the summary box 50 mm (about 2 inches) high, which are the figures commonly given for the method. You can change both in the form.

Paper and pen

Use the cue column after the lecture, not during it. The most helpful cues are questions you could answer from the notes. Print a stack and keep one topic per sheet so you can reorder them. Summarise in your own words; copying a line from the notes does not test understanding.

How much fits with these settings

Using the settings this page starts with (margin 10 mm), a sheet holds: A4 portrait: printable area 190 by 277 mm; A5 portrait: printable area 128 by 190 mm; A3 portrait: printable area 277 by 400 mm; Letter portrait: printable area 195 by 259 mm. Change the numbers in the form and the preview updates; the line under the preview states the count for your own settings. Landscape swaps the two numbers.

Common questions

Why rule the notes area?

Lines keep handwriting neat; set the spacing to 0 or high if you prefer a plain area.
