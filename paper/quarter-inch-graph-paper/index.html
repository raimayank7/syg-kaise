Quarter inch graph paper

Squares of a quarter inch, the common American grid, with a bold line every four squares (one inch).

The US grid

In the United States and Canada graph paper is sold in fractions of an inch. A quarter inch is 6.35 mm. With a bold line every four squares you have a one-inch grid for scale drawings, floor plans and knitting charts that are planned in inches.

Scale drawings

A common scale is one square = one foot, so a 12 ft by 10 ft room is 12 by 10 squares. Draw walls first, then doors and windows, then furniture cut-outs. Cut out paper shapes for furniture to try layouts.

Letter paper

The preset uses US Letter (8.5 by 11 inches) with a 12.7 mm (half inch) margin. Switch to A4 in the form if you are outside North America.

Paper and pen

Use a hard pencil (HB or H) for plotting so lines stay thin and erase cleanly. Mark axes with a ruler and label the unit on each. If the graph will be photocopied, choose black or dark grey for the grid, because pale blue and green can vanish on a copy. Keep a few spare sheets: a first plot often shows the scale was wrong.

How much fits with these settings

Using the settings this page starts with (margin 12.7 mm), a sheet holds: A4 portrait: 29 across and 42 down (1218 squares); A5 portrait: 19 across and 29 down (551 squares); A3 portrait: 42 across and 62 down (2604 squares); Letter portrait: 30 across and 40 down (1200 squares). Change the numbers in the form and the preview updates; the line under the preview states the count for your own settings. Landscape swaps the two numbers.

Common questions

Is it exactly 1/4 inch?

6.35 mm is exactly a quarter of an inch, so the grid matches a ruler marked in inches.
