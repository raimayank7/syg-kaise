Printable hexagonal paper

A honeycomb of regular hexagons, drawn in millimetres. Set the cell size and print.

Uses

  • Organic chemistry: drawing benzene rings and fused rings.
  • Board and war game maps on hex grids.
  • Tessellation and pattern design.
  • Quilting and mosaic planning.

Cell size

The number is the distance from the centre of a hexagon to a corner, in millimetres. A value of 6 gives a cell about 10 mm across the flats, which fits a ring with a label inside. Use 8 to 12 for game maps and 4 to 5 for fine chemistry notes.

Printing

Print at actual size. Hexagons are drawn clipped at the margin, so a thin partial row appears at the edges; set a larger margin if you want clean borders.

Choosing a cell size for a purpose

For organic chemistry notes a radius of 4 to 5 mm gives rings small enough to fit several on a line yet large enough for atom labels. For wargame or role-playing maps, 8 to 12 mm lets a counter sit in a cell. For tessellation art, try 10 mm and colour cells in repeating patterns. Print a trial sheet, draw one ring or place one counter, and then print the whole pack: hexagon size is more about hand and pen than about the page.

Paper and pen

Print on slightly heavier paper if you will colour in cells. Use the same cell size across a set of sheets so maps and diagrams join up. For game maps, number the rows and columns along the margin so any cell can be named.

How much fits with these settings

Using the settings this page starts with (margin 10 mm), a sheet holds: A4 portrait: printable area 190 by 277 mm; A5 portrait: printable area 128 by 190 mm; A3 portrait: printable area 277 by 400 mm; Letter portrait: printable area 195 by 259 mm. Change the numbers in the form and the preview updates; the line under the preview states the count for your own settings. Landscape swaps the two numbers.

Common questions

Are the hexagons regular?

Yes. All six sides and angles are equal; rows are offset by half a cell.
