RERA advance limit and the 70% account: sections 13 and 4(2)(l)(D)

Two rules protect your money before possession: a ten per cent cap on advance before an agreement for sale, and a separate account for seventy per cent of what buyers pay.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (Act 16 of 2016, the India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 9 June 2023). We read the Act only. The forms, fees and interest rates are "as prescribed" by each State’s rules, which we did not read, so we state none of them. Check your State’s rules and portal before you file.

The ten per cent limit: section 13(1)

A promoter shall not accept a sum of more than ten per cent of the cost of the apartment, plot or building as an advance payment or an application fee from a person without first entering into a written agreement for sale with that person and registering the agreement for sale under any law for the time being in force.

What the agreement for sale must contain: section 13(2)

The agreement shall be in the prescribed form and shall specify the particulars of development of the project including the construction of building and apartments, along with specifications and internal and external development works, the dates and manner of payments towards the cost, the date on which possession is to be handed over, and the rates of interest payable by the promoter to the allottee and by the allottee to the promoter in case of default. We read the opening of the sub-section only.

The separate account: section 4(2)(l)(D)

Among the declarations that go with the registration application, the promoter declares that seventy per cent of the amounts realised for the project from the allottees, from time to time, shall be deposited in a separate account in a scheduled bank to cover the cost of construction and the land cost, and shall be used only for that purpose. The proviso says the promoter shall withdraw amounts from that account to cover the cost of the project in proportion to the percentage of completion. We read the opening of the second proviso only.

What this means for you

Do not hand over more than ten per cent before the agreement for sale is signed and registered. After that, ask for the payment schedule in the agreement and check the project’s quarterly updates on the Authority’s website.

Common questions

Can a builder take 20% as booking money?

Section 13(1) says not more than ten per cent as advance or application fee without a registered written agreement for sale.

Where does the money I pay go?

Seventy per cent of the amounts realised is to be kept in a separate scheduled-bank account for construction and land costs, according to the promoter’s declaration under section 4(2)(l)(D).
