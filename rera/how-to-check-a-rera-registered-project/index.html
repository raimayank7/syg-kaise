How to check a RERA project: what the promoter must publish

The Act makes the promoter put project details on the Authority’s website and show the registration number in advertisements. This page lists what to look for.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (Act 16 of 2016, the India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 9 June 2023). We read the Act only. The forms, fees and interest rates are "as prescribed" by each State’s rules, which we did not read, so we state none of them. Check your State’s rules and portal before you file.

A web page for the project: section 11(1)

The promoter shall create a web page on the Authority’s website and enter all details of the proposed project for public viewing, including (a) details of the registration granted; (b) a quarterly up-to-date list of the number and types of apartments or plots booked; (c) the quarterly list of garages booked; (d) the quarterly list of approvals taken and those pending after the commencement certificate; (e) the quarterly status of the project; and (f) other information the Authority’s regulations specify.

The registration number in the advertisement: section 11(2)

The advertisement or prospectus shall mention prominently the website address of the Authority, where all details of the registered project have been entered, and include the registration number obtained from the Authority.

At booking: section 11(3)

At the time of booking and issue of the allotment letter the promoter shall make available to the allottee the sanctioned plans, layout plans and specifications approved by the competent authority (by display at the site or as the regulations specify) and the stage-wise time schedule of completion, including provisions for civic infrastructure like water, sanitation and electricity.

If the advertisement was false: section 12

Where a person makes an advance or deposit on the basis of the information in a notice, advertisement or prospectus, or on the basis of a model apartment, plot or building, and sustains loss or damage by reason of an incorrect or false statement in it, the promoter shall compensate the person as the Act provides. If the person wishes to withdraw from the project, the proviso says he shall be returned his entire investment with interest at the prescribed rate and the compensation provided.

A short checklist

  • Find the registration number in the advertisement and search it on your State’s RERA portal.
  • Compare the plan, specifications and completion date on the portal with the brochure and the draft agreement.
  • Look at the quarterly updates: are they current?
  • Ask for the sanctioned plans before you sign (section 11(3)(a)).
  • Do not pay more than the Act allows before an agreement for sale: see our page on advances.

Common questions

Where is the project page?

Section 11(1) says on the website of the Authority of your State. Our state portals page lists the ones we could read.

Is a project without a registration number safe?

Under section 3(1) a project that needs registration cannot be advertised or sold without it; unless an exemption in section 3(2) applies, ask why there is no number.
