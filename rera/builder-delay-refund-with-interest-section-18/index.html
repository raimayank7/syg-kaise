Builder delay: refund or interest under section 18

If a promoter fails to deliver on time, section 18 gives you a choice. This page sets it out, with section 19(4) and the five-year defect rule in section 14(3).

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (Act 16 of 2016, the India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 9 June 2023). We read the Act only. The forms, fees and interest rates are "as prescribed" by each State’s rules, which we did not read, so we state none of them. Check your State’s rules and portal before you file.

The two situations: section 18(1)

If the promoter fails to complete or is unable to give possession of an apartment, plot or building (a) in accordance with the terms of the agreement for sale or, as the case may be, duly completed by the date specified in it; or (b) due to discontinuance of his business as a developer on account of suspension or revocation of the registration or for any other reason, he is liable on demand to the allottees, if the allottee wishes to withdraw from the project, without prejudice to any other remedy, to return the amount received with interest at such rate as may be prescribed, including compensation in the manner the Act provides.

If you want to stay: the proviso

Where an allottee does not intend to withdraw from the project, the promoter shall pay interest for every month of delay, till handing over of possession, at such rate as may be prescribed.

Defective title: section 18(2)

The promoter shall compensate the allottees for any loss caused to them due to a defective title of the land on which the project is being developed or has been developed, in the manner the Act provides, and the claim for compensation under this sub-section is not barred by limitation provided under any law for the time being in force.

Your right as an allottee: section 19(4)

The allottee is entitled to claim a refund of the amount paid along with interest at the prescribed rate and compensation in the manner the Act provides, if the promoter fails to comply or is unable to give possession in accordance with the terms of the agreement for sale or due to discontinuance of his business as a developer on account of suspension or revocation of his registration.

Defects after possession: section 14(3)

If a structural defect or any other defect in workmanship, quality or provision of services, or any other obligation of the promoter under the agreement for sale, is brought to the promoter’s notice within five years from the date of handing over possession, it is the promoter’s duty to rectify it without further charge within thirty days. If the promoter fails to do so within that time, the aggrieved allottees are entitled to appropriate compensation as the Act provides.

What we do not state

The rate of interest is "as prescribed", so it is in your State’s rules, which we did not read. A demand letter first, then a complaint to the Authority under section 31, is the usual path; our sample letter and the complaint page follow it.

Common questions

Can I get a refund if the builder is late?

Section 18(1) says on demand, if you wish to withdraw, the promoter must return the amount received with interest at the prescribed rate and compensation as the Act provides.

What if I do not want to withdraw?

The proviso to section 18(1) says the promoter pays you interest for every month of delay till possession is handed over, at the prescribed rate.
