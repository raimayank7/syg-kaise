Demand letter to a promoter for delay (sample)

A written demand to the promoter, sent before a complaint to the Authority, recording your dates and your choice under section 18.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

To, The Promoter / Managing Director, [Name of the builder or company] [Address] Date: [Date] Subject: Delay in possession of [Apartment / Plot / Unit number] in [Project name], RERA registration no. [Registration number] Sir/Madam, 1. I booked [Apartment / Plot / Unit number] in the project [Project name] and entered into an agreement for sale dated [Date of agreement] (registered on [Date of registration]). I have paid Rs [Amount] up to [Date] against the total price of Rs [Total price]. 2. The agreement states that possession would be handed over by [Date promised in the agreement]. It has not been handed over as of the date of this letter. 3. Under section 18(1) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, [Choose one: I wish to withdraw from the project and I demand return of the amount paid with interest at the rate prescribed, with compensation as the Act provides. / I do not wish to withdraw from the project and I demand payment of interest for every month of delay till possession is handed over, at the rate prescribed.] 4. Please pay the amount due and confirm in writing within [Number] days of this letter. If the matter is not settled I will be free to file a complaint under section 31 of the Act and to take any other remedy open to me. Copies of the agreement for sale, my payment receipts and the booking letter are enclosed. Yours faithfully, ______________________ [Your full name] [Your address] [Your phone or e-mail] Enclosures: [List the agreement, receipts and letters]

What this sample is for

A written demand to the promoter, sent before a complaint to the Authority, recording your dates and your choice under section 18.

Which parts of the Act it follows

  • Section 18(1): on demand, return of the amount with interest at the prescribed rate and compensation if the allottee withdraws; interest for every month of delay till handing over if he stays.
  • Section 19(4): the allottee’s right to claim a refund with interest and compensation.
  • Section 31(1): any aggrieved person may complain to the Authority or the adjudicating officer; section 31(2): form, manner and fees as prescribed.
  • The rate of interest is as prescribed in your State’s rules, which we did not read.

Check before you send

  • Use the dates in your agreement for sale and your receipts; quote the registration number from the portal.
  • Choose one of the two options in paragraph 3 and delete the other.
  • Send by registered post or courier with proof and keep a copy.
  • Check your State portal for the complaint form and fee before you file.

Sources

Read on 13 November 2026: the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (Act 16 of 2016, the India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 9 June 2023). We read the Act only. The forms, fees and interest rates are "as prescribed" by each State’s rules, which we did not read, so we state none of them. Check your State’s rules and portal before you file.
