Your rights when the police arrest you: BNSS sections 35, 36, 38, 47, 48

The Sanhita limits arrest without a warrant and lists what the police must do. This page follows sections 35, 36, 38, 47 and 48(1) and Article 22(1).

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

When arrest without a warrant is allowed: section 35(1)

For a cognizable offence punishable with imprisonment that may be less than seven years or extend to seven years, the police officer may arrest only if he has reason to believe on the basis of the complaint, information or suspicion that the person committed the offence and is satisfied that arrest is necessary to prevent a further offence, for proper investigation, to prevent disappearance or tampering with evidence, to prevent inducement, threat or promise to witnesses, or because the person’s presence in court cannot otherwise be ensured. He shall record his reasons in writing, and the proviso says he shall record in writing his reasons for not arresting when arrest is not required. For offences punishable with more than seven years or death, section 35(1)(c) allows arrest on credible information and reason to believe. We read the list of other cases in section 35(1) only in outline.

A notice instead of arrest: section 35(3) to (6)

Where arrest is not required under section 35(1), the police officer shall issue a notice directing the person to appear before him or at another specified place. The person must comply. If he complies and continues to comply, he shall not be arrested for the offence in the notice unless, for reasons to be recorded, the officer thinks he ought to be arrested. If he fails to comply or is unwilling to identify himself, the officer may, subject to orders of a competent court, arrest him.

Elderly or infirm persons: section 35(7)

No arrest shall be made without the prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police in case of an offence punishable with imprisonment of less than three years where the person is infirm or above sixty years of age.

How the police must arrest: section 36

Every police officer making an arrest shall bear an accurate, visible and clear identification of his name; prepare a memorandum of arrest attested by at least one witness who is a member of the family of the person arrested or a respectable member of the locality, and countersigned by the person arrested; and, unless the memorandum is attested by a family member, inform the person arrested that he has a right to have a relative, friend or other person named by him informed of the arrest.

What you must be told: section 47 and Article 22(1)

Section 47(1) says every police officer arresting without a warrant shall forthwith communicate to the person full particulars of the offence or other grounds for the arrest. Section 47(2) says that where a person other than one accused of a non-bailable offence is arrested without a warrant, he shall be told he is entitled to be released on bail and may arrange sureties. Article 22(1) of the Constitution says no person who is arrested shall be detained in custody without being informed, as soon as may be, of the grounds for the arrest, nor be denied the right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of his choice.

Informing family: section 48(1)

Every police officer making an arrest shall forthwith give information about the arrest and the place where the person is held to any of his relatives, friends or other persons disclosed or nominated by the arrested person, and also to the designated police officer in the district.

An advocate during questioning: section 38

When a person is arrested and interrogated by the police, he is entitled to meet an advocate of his choice during interrogation, though not throughout interrogation.

Common questions

Can the police arrest me for any offence without a warrant?

Not freely. Section 35(1) sets conditions and requires reasons in writing, and section 35(3) to (6) provides for a notice of appearance when arrest is not required.

Can I call a lawyer?

Article 22(1) says you shall not be denied the right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of your choice, and section 38 lets you meet an advocate of your choice during interrogation, though not throughout it.
