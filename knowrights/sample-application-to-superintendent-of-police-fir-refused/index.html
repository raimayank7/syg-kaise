Application to the Superintendent of Police when the police refuse to record an FIR

A written application to the Superintendent of Police, sent by post, when the officer in charge of a police station has refused to record your information about a cognizable offence.

To, The Superintendent of Police, [District or city] Date: [Date] Subject: Refusal to record information about a cognizable offence — application under section 173(4) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 Sir/Madam, 1. On [Date] at about [Time] I went to [Name of police station] and gave information to the officer in charge about an offence that took place on [Date of incident] at [Place of incident]. 2. The substance of my information is: [Describe in a few numbered lines what happened, who did it if known, and any witnesses or documents]. 3. The officer in charge [Name or description of the officer, if known] refused to record my information [and gave this reason: Reason given, if any]. 4. I am aggrieved by this refusal. Under section 173(4) of the Sanhita I am sending the substance of my information in writing and by post, and I request you to investigate the case yourself or direct an investigation by a subordinate police officer, if you are satisfied that it discloses the commission of a cognizable offence. 5. Please give me a copy of the information as recorded, free of cost, as section 173(2) provides. Documents enclosed: [List any papers, photographs or messages] Yours faithfully, ______________________ [Your full name] [Your address] [Your phone or e-mail] Sent by [Registered post / Speed post] on [Date]; receipt no. [Receipt number]

What this sample is for

A written application to the Superintendent of Police, sent by post, when the officer in charge of a police station has refused to record your information about a cognizable offence.

Which parts of the Act it follows

  • Section 173(1): information of a cognizable offence may be given orally or electronically to the officer in charge of a police station.
  • Section 173(2): a copy of the information as recorded shall be given forthwith, free of cost, to the informant or the victim.
  • Section 173(4): a person aggrieved by a refusal to record the information may send its substance in writing and by post to the Superintendent of Police, who, if satisfied that it discloses a cognizable offence, shall investigate or direct an investigation; failing which the person may apply to the Magistrate.

Check before you send

  • Say only what you know. Do not name a person as guilty without a reason you can state.
  • Send it by registered or speed post and keep the receipt: the section speaks of sending it by post.
  • Keep a copy of the letter and note the date the police station refused.
  • If nothing happens, a lawyer can help you apply to the Magistrate under the last words of section 173(4).

Sources

Read on 13 November 2026: the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 10 September 2024) and Article 22 of the Constitution of India (India Code copy). We read only the sections named on each page. We did not read the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, court judgments, state police rules or any amendment after 2023, and the India Code copy of the Sanhita has at least one section whose printed text looks repeated, which we avoided.
