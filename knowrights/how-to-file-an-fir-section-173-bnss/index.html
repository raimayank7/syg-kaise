How to file an FIR: section 173 of the BNSS

Section 173 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 sets how information about a serious (cognizable) offence is recorded. This page follows it.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 10 September 2024) and Article 22 of the Constitution of India (India Code copy). We read only the sections named on each page. We did not read the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, court judgments, state police rules or any amendment after 2023, and the India Code copy of the Sanhita has at least one section whose printed text looks repeated, which we avoided. This is general information, not advice for your case: speak to a lawyer, and to the legal services authority if you cannot afford one.

Where and how: section 173(1)

Every information relating to the commission of a cognizable offence, irrespective of the area where the offence is committed, may be given orally or by electronic communication to an officer in charge of a police station. If it is given orally, it shall be reduced to writing by that officer or under his direction and read over to the informant, and every such information, whether written or reduced to writing, shall be signed by the person giving it. If it is given by electronic communication, it shall be taken on record on being signed within three days by the person giving it, and its substance entered in a book kept in the form the State Government prescribes.

Cases involving women

The first proviso says that if the information is given by the woman against whom an offence under the listed sections of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 is alleged to have been committed or attempted, it shall be recorded by a woman police officer or any woman officer. The second proviso adds that where the woman is temporarily or permanently mentally or physically disabled, the information shall be recorded by a police officer at her residence or a convenient place of her choice, in the presence of an interpreter or a special educator, the recording shall be videographed, and the police officer shall get her statement recorded by a Magistrate as soon as possible. We did not read the offences those listed sections describe.

You get a copy, free: section 173(2)

A copy of the information as recorded shall be given forthwith, free of cost, to the informant or the victim.

Offences punishable with 3 to under 7 years: section 173(3)

On information of a cognizable offence punishable with imprisonment of three years or more but less than seven years, the officer in charge may, with the prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police and considering the nature and gravity of the offence, either proceed to conduct a preliminary enquiry within fourteen days to ascertain whether there is a prima facie case, or proceed with the investigation when there is a prima facie case. This is subject to section 175, which we did not read.

If the police refuse: section 173(4)

A person aggrieved by a refusal of the officer in charge to record the information may send its substance in writing and by post to the Superintendent of Police concerned. If satisfied that the information discloses a cognizable offence, the Superintendent shall either investigate the case himself or direct an investigation by a subordinate police officer, and such officer shall have all the powers of an officer in charge of the police station in relation to that offence. If that fails, the aggrieved person may make an application to the Magistrate. Our sample letter to the Superintendent of Police follows this.

What to keep

Keep the copy you are given, the date and time you gave the information and the name of the officer. If you sent anything by post, keep the postal receipt.

Common questions

Can I give the information by e-mail or SMS?

Section 173(1)(ii) allows electronic communication, which shall be taken on record on being signed within three days by the person giving it. Ask the station how it takes electronic reports.

Is the FIR copy free?

Section 173(2) says a copy shall be given forthwith, free of cost, to the informant or the victim.

Do I need to file in the area where it happened?

Section 173(1) says “irrespective of the area where the offence is committed”.
