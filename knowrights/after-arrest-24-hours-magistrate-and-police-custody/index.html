After an arrest: 24 hours, the Magistrate and custody

A person arrested must be taken to a Magistrate quickly, and custody is limited. This page follows Article 22(2) and section 187(1) and (2).

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

The 24-hour rule: Article 22(2)

Every person who is arrested and detained in custody shall be produced before the nearest Magistrate within twenty-four hours of such arrest, excluding the time necessary for the journey from the place of arrest to the court of the Magistrate, and no such person shall be detained in custody beyond that period without the authority of a Magistrate. Clause (3) says clauses (1) and (2) do not apply to an enemy alien or to a person arrested or detained under a law providing for preventive detention.

When the investigation cannot finish in 24 hours: section 187(1)

Whenever a person is arrested and detained and it appears that the investigation cannot be completed within the twenty-four hours fixed by section 58, and there are grounds for believing that the accusation or information is well-founded, the officer in charge of the police station or the investigating officer (not below the rank of sub-inspector) shall forthwith transmit to the nearest Magistrate a copy of the entries in the diary relating to the case and at the same time forward the accused to the Magistrate. We do not state what section 58 itself says because the copy we read printed its text unclearly.

What the Magistrate may authorise: section 187(2)

The Magistrate, irrespective of whether he has jurisdiction to try the case, may after considering whether the person has not been released on bail or his bail has been cancelled, authorise from time to time the detention of the accused in such custody as he thinks fit for a term not exceeding fifteen days in the whole, or in parts, at any time during the initial forty days or sixty days out of the detention period of sixty days or ninety days, as provided in sub-section (3). We did not read sub-section (3) in full, so we do not explain which offences get sixty and which ninety days.

What you can ask for

At the first appearance you can tell the Magistrate of any ill-treatment, ask for your advocate or free legal aid, and ask for bail where the offence is bailable (see our bail page). Ask that what you say is recorded.

Keep a record

Ask a relative to note the time of arrest, the police station and the name of the officer, and to keep the memorandum of arrest.

Common questions

Is a person always freed after 24 hours?

No. Article 22(2) says no detention beyond 24 hours without the authority of a Magistrate, and section 187 lets a Magistrate authorise custody in the way described.

Does the 24 hours include travel?

Article 22(2) excludes the time necessary for the journey from the place of arrest to the court of the Magistrate.
