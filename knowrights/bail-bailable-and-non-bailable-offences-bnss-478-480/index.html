Bail: sections 478, 479 and 480 of the BNSS

Section 478 gives bail as of right for bailable offences; section 480 sets rules for non-bailable ones; section 479 limits how long an undertrial can be held. This page follows them.

Sample, not legal advice. This page explains what the Act says in plain words and offers a general sample. It is not advice for your case. Offices, courts, registrars and notaries may ask for their own wording, forms or stamp paper, and the law of your state may add rules. Ask the office concerned or a lawyer before you sign.

Sources

Sources read on 13 November 2026: the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (India Code copy, read through an Internet Archive snapshot of 10 September 2024) and Article 22 of the Constitution of India (India Code copy). We read only the sections named on each page. We did not read the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, court judgments, state police rules or any amendment after 2023, and the India Code copy of the Sanhita has at least one section whose printed text looks repeated, which we avoided. Bailable offences: section 478(1)

When a person other than one accused of a non-bailable offence is arrested or detained without warrant by an officer in charge of a police station, or appears or is brought before a Court, and is prepared at any time while in custody or at any stage of the proceeding to give bail, he shall be released on bail. The proviso says the officer or Court may, and shall if the person is indigent and unable to furnish surety, instead of taking a bail bond discharge him on his executing a bond for his appearance. The Explanation says that where a person cannot give a bail bond within a week of arrest, that is a sufficient ground to presume he is indigent.

If you skip your date: section 478(2)

Where a person has failed to comply with the conditions of the bond as regards the time and place of attendance, the Court may refuse to release him on bail when he later appears or is brought in custody in the same case.

Non-bailable offences: section 480(1)

A person accused of a non-bailable offence arrested without warrant by the police or brought before a Court other than the High Court or Court of Session may be released on bail, but not if there appear reasonable grounds for believing that he is guilty of an offence punishable with death or imprisonment for life, and not if the offence is cognizable and he was previously convicted of an offence punishable with death, life imprisonment or seven years or more, or convicted on two or more earlier occasions of a cognizable offence punishable with three years or more but less than seven. The first proviso lets the Court release a child, a woman or a sick or infirm person despite this; the second proviso lets it release a person with a previous conviction for any other special reason if it is just and proper. We read the opening of the section only.

Release after part of the sentence: section 479(1)

Where a person has, during investigation, inquiry or trial of an offence under any law (not one punishable with death or life imprisonment) undergone detention up to one-half of the maximum period of imprisonment for that offence, the Court shall release him on bail. The first proviso says a first-time offender who has never been convicted of any offence shall be released on bond after detention up to one-third of the maximum. The second proviso lets the Court, after hearing the Public Prosecutor and recording reasons, order longer detention or release on bail bond instead of bond. The third proviso says no such person shall be detained for more than the maximum period of imprisonment for the offence. We read sub-section (1) only, not the later sub-sections.

Which offences are bailable?

Whether an offence is bailable is shown in the First Schedule to the Sanhita, which we did not read, and in the punishment sections of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, which we did not read. Ask your advocate; do not assume.

Common questions

Is bail a right?

For a bailable offence, section 478(1) says the person shall be released on bail if prepared to give it. For a non-bailable offence, section 480 says he may be released, subject to the limits above.

Can a poor person be released without a surety?

The proviso to section 478(1) says the officer or Court shall discharge an indigent person who cannot furnish surety on a bond for appearance, and the Explanation treats inability to give a bail bond within a week of arrest as sufficient ground to presume indigence.
