How to build habits with a tracker

A tracker works only if the habit is small and the page is where you will see it. Here is how to set one up.

Pick small habits

The best habit to start is one you can do in two minutes on your worst day. "Read one page", "do five squats", "write one line". Once it is automatic, extend it. A habit that needs motivation will fail on a tired day; a habit that is tiny will not.

Attach to a cue

A cue is something that already happens: waking up, tea, lunch, switching off the lamp. Write the habit as "After I ___, I will ___" and put the tracker where the cue happens, for example next to the kettle.

Use the page

  • Print the month and pin it where you will pass it daily.
  • Fill the circle the moment you finish.
  • Never fill a circle in advance.
  • At month end, count the filled circles per habit and decide to keep, grow or drop it.

When you miss

Do not make up for two missed days by doing double. Simply do the next scheduled one. If you keep missing, the habit is too big or the time is wrong. Shrink it and try a different time. This is guidance for ordinary habits, not for medical or addiction concerns, where a professional should help.

A first month

Week one: choose two tiny habits and fill the circles every day, even if the habit lasts one minute. Week two: look at the circles; if one habit has many blanks, make it smaller or change its time. Week three: add a third habit only if the first two are going well. Week four: count the circles, write what helped and what did not on the back of the page, and plan next month. A month of filled circles is a record you can see, which is more motivating than a good intention.

Common questions

How long does a habit take to form?

It varies by person and habit; there is no fixed number of days. Judge by how easy it feels, not by the calendar.
