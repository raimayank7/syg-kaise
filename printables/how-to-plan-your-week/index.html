How to plan your week on paper

Ten minutes of planning on Sunday saves hours during the week. This routine uses the weekly planner page.

The routine

  • Look back (2 minutes): what got done, what did not, and why?
  • Look ahead (3 minutes): list fixed commitments in the day boxes: classes, meetings, appointments, pickups.
  • Choose (3 minutes): write the three outcomes that would make the week a success in the priorities box.
  • Slot (2 minutes): place one priority task into a free part of each of the next days.

Leave slack

People habitually plan every hour and then feel behind by Tuesday. Keep about a quarter of the week free for interruptions and recovery. A plan that has no slack is a wish.

Review on Friday

Spend five minutes on Friday afternoon. Tick what is done, carry over what matters and drop the rest. Notice what took longer than you expected and plan longer for similar tasks next week.

Paper and phone

Phones remind; paper commits. Use your phone for alarms and for things with a time, and the printed page for the shape of the week.

A sample week

On Sunday you write three results: finish the project report, book a dentist appointment, and prepare Monday's lunch box. Then place fixed items: Tuesday meeting, Thursday class. Slot "report first draft" into Monday morning, "book dentist" into Wednesday lunch and "lunch box" into Sunday evening. Leave Friday afternoon free to catch up. By Friday you can see what was done and what moved, and next week's plan starts from that. The point is not a perfect schedule but a visible one, so that a surprise on Wednesday changes the plan on paper instead of in your head.

Common questions

Where should the planner live?

On your desk or wall, in sight, rather than in a drawer.
