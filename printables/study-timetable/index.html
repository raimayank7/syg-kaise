Study timetable for exams

A seven-day grid with six study slots a day, for planning revision.

How to plan revision

Count the days to your exam and list your subjects. Give each subject slots in proportion to how weak you are in it and how many marks it carries, then write them into the grid. Put the hardest subject in the slot when you are most alert, and leave at least one slot a day for a short revision of the day before.

Healthy habits

  • Study in blocks of 45 to 60 minutes with a 10 minute break.
  • Keep one half-day a week free.
  • Sleep, meals and a walk are part of the plan, not extras.
  • Test yourself with past questions rather than only re-reading.

Using the grid

Rows are slots (period 1 to 6) and columns are the seven days. Write times beside the slot numbers by hand. Print a fresh copy each week and tick off completed slots.

Ideas for adapting it

Make two copies: one for the wall and one for your bag. Write a colour key in the margin. If your school changes the plan mid-term, reprint rather than crossing out. Teachers can print a timetable per class and one per teacher and compare them to find clashes before the term begins.

Printing this page

The page opens as A4 landscape with a 10 mm margin. Change any of these in the form: A3 gives more writing room, A5 fits a folder, Letter suits North American printers. Press Print and choose Actual size so the sheet matches the preview proportions, or choose Save as PDF to keep it. Print a single test page first; check that nothing is cut at the edge before you print a pack.

Common questions

Is it the same as the class timetable?

It uses the same grid with seven days and six slots, and a study title. Change the numbers if you need more.
